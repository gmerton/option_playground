> already measured as having no edge.** Breitstein separates the two questions most reviewers
> merge — *is he really trading?* (yes: public statements, an accountant's report, an FTC fine as
> deterrent) and *is that record evidence about the method?* (no: livestreaming to thousands in
> sub-$100M-float names is a reflexivity engine). ⚠ But he spends 13 of 22 minutes endorsing an

## 1. The rules, as relayed

### Universe and selection (Breitstein paraphrasing, [01:31]–[02:15])

- **Small-cap / micro-cap momentum day trader**, "mostly looking for intraday continuation."
- Only stocks making **extreme moves** — "because that is where the most edge is."
- Scanner gates: **float under 100 million shares**, **strong daily charts**, **high relative
  volume**, and **a catalyst** — "earnings, an FDA announcement, or other breaking news."
- "The daily list is usually tiny after he screens the market" — a handful of in-play names.
- **Direction: continuation longs, not shorts.** Breitstein endorses this explicitly for the
  niche: "statistically, yes, these do tend to mean revert and come back to Earth. But in doing
  so, many traders blow up on the stocks that end up going up hundreds or even thousands of
  percents higher" [02:37].
- Heavy use of **scanners and automation**. Breitstein: "it is inexcusable to miss an A+ setup for
  your trading style" [03:30].

### Daily P&L risk rules (Cameron's own words)

> "I don't like to cap my green days. I want to cap my red days. I have to cap my loss days. But
> I don't want to cap my green days because what about days like today where, you know, if I'm
> just willing to dig deep and trade a little longer, I end up tripling or quadrupling, whatever
> it might be, my daily goal." [05:20]

**Giveback rule:** Breitstein relays it as "usually around 30% off his highs, is when he will turn
it off and call it a day" [06:37]. The clip shows the rule live: up $52.5k after peaking at $72k,
having given back ~$20k, he stops.

**Daily loss limit** (his words):

> "I don't really have at this point a very hard set max loss on my account of where I have to
> stop. I generally say that I don't want to lose more than I can make in one good day. Because
> when you lose more than you… make on your typical day, that's where I personally get really
> triggered and I get really frustrated." [11:57]

**Consecutive-loser rule** (his words):

> "Three strikes, you're out. It's baseball, you know, but why why even go for three? Why not just
> cut it at two? Like, at a certain point, I think it makes sense to accept defeat." [09:49]

**The tilt loop, self-diagnosed** — the single best passage in the video:

> "The feeling of the frustration is so strong, you don't want to feel frustrated anymore… I'm red
> $12,000. So, if I can make 12,000, I won't feel frustrated anymore. So, now, that's only 20 cents
> on a 60,000 share position… 60,000 shares, you lose 5 10 cents maybe on slippage getting in,
> another 5 10 cents on slippage getting out. Next thing you know, I'm down 25,000 on the day. And
> actually, statistically, **I have an 80% chance of doubling my loss after I go below max loss.**"
> [10:16]–[10:49]

### Execution

**Tape reading in thin names** [11:23]: buy orders filling easily against hidden sellers, then
nothing on the bid to absorb what he just bought. Breitstein: "in his niche of small caps, the
ability to read the tape like this and recognize discrepancies between buyers and sellers is so
critical."

## 3. What Breitstein rejects or qualifies

1. **Fully calling it a day** [07:16] — "If the market is open and I haven't hit a daily risk
   limit, my butt is generally in the seat." His version: a giveback rule that makes him
   *hands-off* (up 100K → willing to give back 25K, not all the way to the daily loss limit),
   not a hard stop, "unless something truly exceptional happens."
2. **The soft loss limit** [12:40] — "every single trader should have some type of **hard-coded**
   loss limit just in case. Because when we are at our worst, we can spiral and one bad day can
   ruin a decade-long career."
3. **The niche itself, on capacity** [03:02] — he personally quit small-cap momentum over
   scalability: "you can only put so much capital behind a name that might only trade a few
   million shares per day while trading for a low share price."
4. ⭐⭐ **The asterisk — the reason the video exists** [14:36]–[20:52]. Livestreaming to thousands
   of chat-room members in thin, reflexive microcaps is a structural advantage that the broker
   statements do not capture:
   > "Ross has the ability to light the spark, his chat room provides the gasoline for the move,
   > then in times of hot markets, the rest of the world is the rocket fuel." [16:16]

   > "My issue is that once you combine a legitimate momentum trader with a giant live audience in
   > a niche that is already reflexive, thin, and crowded, especially one that is often entering
   > these trades premarket when they are most easily influenced, you create a structural advantage
   > that public broker statements do not fully explain." [16:37]

   He is careful about the accusation he is *not* making: "I am not accusing Ross Cameron of any
   pumping and dumping, but I do think when he enters a trade, he's doing it on its own merits and
   all of it is legal" [15:44]; "I cannot, nor can anyone, easily prove what percentage of his
   profits come from his audience chasing him" [19:59]. He also states his own policy: "I never
   share any of my own trading that could influence the position" [18:00].

   **⚠ The evidence is hearsay and he labels it as such.** Four unrelated senior traders, 10+
   years each, each running their own communities (i.e. competitors), each having been inside
   Cameron's: **all four** believe the majority of Cameron's profits come from the piggyback
   tailwind; **two of the four** believe he would not be profitable without it [20:19]–[20:41].
   Breitstein's own read: "I would not be surprised if the tailwind is responsible for a majority
   of his trading profits" [20:14].
5. **The landing** [21:14]–[21:47]: the Bonds/McGwire asterisk analogy. "Ross may be a very good
   trader, but also maybe not since he is not trading under laboratory conditions." And the
   generalizable version: "**even when someone is legitimate, that does not automatically mean
   their edge is clearly replicable by you.**"

## 4. ⚠ The repo's verdict, rule by rule

| **Same-day round trip** | ⛔ **The negative bucket in both books** | **Exit-timing study** (2026-09-18): our pool scalp **−0.13R** vs trail **+0.89R**; Gabe's own **278 same-day cycles −$8.3k, 19% win**, and the alert-triggered ones were no better. |

### ⚠ The honest caveat on the negative verdict — state it every time

Our universe is liquid Minervini-style names with ADR and dollar-volume gates. **No sub-$100M-float
microcap has ever appeared in any result in this repo** — the same structural blind spot that
[`ipo-strategies.md`](ipo-strategies.md) lands on. And our cost model (IBKR $0.65/contract + 25% of
the bid/ask) is calibrated for options on liquid names, not for crossing a spread on a $4 stock at
60,000 shares. **Stage A does not refute Cameron in Cameron's own universe.** It refutes the
version of Cameron that this repo's data and account could trade — which, per **precision over
recall**, is the only version that matters to us. Per the KB's ⚠⚠ timeframe rule: *a null result
on our bars and our universe is not a refutation of his.*

## 5. ⚠ Prop / structural dependency — the whole point of the video

- **Depends on:** a live audience of thousands in a thin-float niche. This is not prop
  infrastructure but it is the same *kind* of thing — a structural advantage that does not travel
  with the method. Also: microcap borrow (for the short side he avoids), sub-penny routing, and a
  capacity ceiling Breitstein says drove him out of the style entirely [03:02].
- **Retail-viable as stated?** **No, and that is the finding.** Whatever part of Cameron's P&L
  comes from being first, visible and copied is unavailable to the follower by construction —
  indeed the follower is the *counterparty* to it. Breitstein makes this explicit [17:34]: "you
  end up with the following of traders that look to piggyback you, but they get worse entries and
  exits."

## 6. Decay risk

**High.** Small-cap momentum reflexivity is the fastest-decaying niche in this repo by the
README's own ranking, and it is conditional on a hot retail tape. Breitstein dates the effect
explicitly to hot markets: "This is especially problematic when these small cap momentum markets
are really hot" [15:55]. The 2021 version of this niche is not the 2026 version.

## 7. Objective assessment

- **The structure is 13 minutes of endorsement and 9 of critique**, and the endorsement carries no
  evidence at all beyond "I did this at Trillium." The critique is where the reasoning lives.
- **The asterisk rests on four anonymous, self-interested sources** plus a prior. He flags it as
  belief rather than proof, which is to his credit — but file it as a **structural hypothesis with
  a named mechanism and no measurement**, the same category this KB is skeptical of when the
  conclusion runs the other way.
- **He raises capacity and then drops it.** At [03:02] capacity limits are why *he* quit — which
  is also the strongest argument that a retail-sized trader could have a real edge there, since
  capacity constraints protect small players. He never returns to it when arguing the tailwind
  explains the P&L.
- **⚠ The only mention of slippage in this entire three-video batch comes from Cameron, not
  Breitstein** — "5 10 cents maybe on slippage getting in, another 5 10 cents on slippage getting
  out" [10:33]. On 60,000 shares that is $6k–$12k of friction on one round trip, named unprompted.
  `U9UZ2U6bozQ` and `eWeGAYvjxh4` never mention the spread once. Given that our studies keep
  finding the spread **is** the P&L, that is a notable inversion of who sounds more professional.
- **The FTC dismissal is too quick** — "from my understanding of the case wasn't really so crazy
  out of pocket. It wasn't that big of a deal" [14:13] — offered without detail, in a video whose
  thesis is that marketing and trading results contaminate each other.

## 8. What's genuinely sound

- ⭐⭐ **"The statements close one debate and open another"** [19:29] is the cleanest one-sentence
  statement of an evidence standard found in any KB in this repo. A verified track record proves
  the *trades*; it proves nothing about the *method* when the conditions are not reproducible.
- ⭐ **This is the repo's own house rule, independently derived.** `feedback_gabes_trades_are_not_evidence`
  exists because a record produced under conditions you cannot reproduce tells you nothing about
  the method. Breitstein reaches the identical principle from the opposite direction — Cameron's
  record is *too contaminated by success conditions* rather than *too small and too poorly
  executed*. Same rule, both tails.
- **The Cameron clips themselves.** An operator describing his own tilt loop in real numbers, his
  giveback rule, his consecutive-loser rule, and a self-measured conditional statistic. That is
  better primary material on loss management than most of the psychology content in this KB.
- **"Even when someone is legitimate, that does not automatically mean their edge is clearly
  replicable by you"** [21:47] — the correct default for every KB in `data/`.

## 9. Testable extractions, ranked

1. ⭐ **The post-max-loss conditional on our own book** — Cameron's "80% chance of doubling my
   loss." Run it against `journal_trades` / `journal_nav`: conditional on a day crossing its loss
   limit, the distribution of the remainder of that day. Cheap, uses only data on disk, and it
   feeds directly into whether a hard-coded limit should be added to the desk routine.
2. **Float as a universe variable.** Never tested here; add it to the Minervini cache gates if the
   Polygon cache carries shares outstanding, and cut existing breakout results by float tercile.
3. **RVOL threshold confirmation.** Cameron says "high relative volume"; rotation study Part II
   says the useful bar is **≥1.8** and that 1.3–1.8 is negative. Already actionable — this is a
   cross-source confirmation of a gate already in `GATE_TIERS`.
4. ⛔ **Do NOT fund intraday microcap data to test his trigger.** Stage A already returned the
   verdict on the transferable version (11,227 fires, every arm negative, random beats the
   trigger) and explicitly concluded "don't fund Polygon history for these detectors." The
   caveat in §4 is a reason to withhold the claim, not a reason to spend.
