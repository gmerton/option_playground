## Claims to verify

- [ ] ⭐⭐ **The core method: intraday continuation longs in high-RVOL catalyst small caps.**
      ⚠ **The repo's closest test says no** — see reactions. Caveat: our universe is not his.
- [ ] ⭐ **Cameron's "80% chance of doubling my loss after I go below max loss"** [10:44]. This is
      a real, specific, self-measured statistic about post-max-loss behaviour and it is the most
      checkable claim in the video. **We can run the analogue on Gabe's own book** off
      `journal_trades` / `journal_nav`: conditional on a day crossing its loss limit, what is the
      distribution of the remaining day? The exit-timing study already found Gabe's 278 same-day
      cycles at −$8.3k / 19% win, which is consistent in spirit but is not the same conditional.
- [ ] **"Float under 100 million shares"** as a gate. The repo has never tested float as a
      universe variable at all — `run_pullback_shorts.py` and the Minervini cache gate on price,
      dollar volume and ADR. Cheap to add if a float field exists in the Polygon cache.
- [ ] **"High relative volume"** as a gate. ✅ **Already tested and it survives, with a threshold
      correction** — the rotation study Part II raised the breakout RVOL gate from 1.2 to 1.8 and
      found **1.3–1.8 is NEGATIVE**. So Cameron is right that RVOL matters and the useful version
      is a *higher* bar than most people use.
- [ ] **Breitstein's estimate that the piggyback tailwind may be "a majority" of Cameron's
      profits** [20:14]. Unfalsifiable from outside. The honest framing is the one he gives.
- [ ] **">$10M in trading profits"** [13:39] — asserted from public broker statements; not
      independently checked here.

## Reactions / conflicts

### ⚠⚠ The repo has tested the closest public analogue of Cameron's trigger and it is negative

Cameron's method is intraday momentum continuation off a gap/catalyst in a thin name. **Stage A**
(2026-09-18) fired the transferable version of exactly that — unfilled-gap reclaim, opening-range
break above the 9 EMA, and level breaks — **11,227 times**, and:

- **every intraday arm returned −0.10 to −0.13R**;
- a **random entry in the same name-day beat the trigger on every arm** (swing trail +0.67R for
  random vs +0.52R for the trigger);
- the conclusion was "no day-trading book, and don't fund Polygon history for these detectors."

Add the **entry study** (2026-09-17): on 2,439 layer-2 name-days, buying the **daily close** beat
*every* intraday entry (paired −0.9 to −2.3pp, t up to −3.4), and intraday stop execution made it
worse. Add the **alert funnel test**: intraday alerts add nothing beyond the daily state (alert
day +3.85% vs no-alert day +4.14%), and an alert-price entry with a session-low stop is −1.5pp
worse than the close entry (t −3.2). Add the **exit-timing study**: same-day exits are the
negative bucket in *both* books.

**Breitstein spends 13 minutes endorsing a method whose transferable core we have measured four
separate ways and found to have no edge.** He does so with zero evidence beyond "I did this at
Trillium and it worked."

⚠ **The honest caveat, and it is real.** Our universe is liquid Minervini-style names with ADR and
dollar-volume gates. **No sub-$100M-float microcap has ever appeared in any result in this repo** —
the same structural blind spot that `ipo-strategies.md` lands on. And our cost model
(IBKR $0.65/contract + 25% of the bid/ask) is calibrated for options on liquid names, not for
crossing a 3-cent spread on a $4 stock at 60,000 shares. So Stage A does **not** refute Cameron in
Cameron's own universe. It refutes the version of Cameron that a retail account with our data
could trade — which, per **precision over recall**, is the only version that matters to us.

### ⭐⭐ The asterisk is this repo's own house rule, independently derived

"Gabe's trades are not evidence" exists because a track record generated under conditions you
cannot reproduce tells you nothing about the method. Breitstein derives the same principle from
the other end: Cameron's statements prove the *trades* are real and prove nothing about the
*edge*, because the conditions (first mover, visible, copied, thin float) are not reproducible —
including by Cameron himself without the room. His line "**the statements close one debate and
open another**" [19:29] is the cleanest one-sentence statement of the repo's evidence standard
found in any KB.

⚠ But note what the evidence for the asterisk actually is: **four anonymous, self-interested
sources** (each "running their own trading communities" — i.e. competitors) plus his own prior.
He labels it as belief, not proof, which is to his credit. File it as a **structural hypothesis
with a named mechanism and no measurement**, exactly the category this KB is supposed to be
skeptical of when the conclusion runs the other way.

### ⚠ "Press the gas when the market is hot" — the repo tested this and could not validate it

Breitstein's loudest endorsement [05:51]–[06:12] is of pressing size when conditions are good, and
he ties it to his exponential-bet-sizing concept. The **breakout regime feedback study**
(2026-09-17) tried to find *any* forward signal for the paying periods and found none: own recent
results, stop-out share, activity level and SPY state were all ≈ nothing; 47% of months were
positive; the top 10% of months carried 68% of positive R; **the only lean found was a low
stop-out month → +0.79R next.** Its conclusion: **"regime management = fixed small sizing, not a
switch."**

So the repo splits Cameron's rule in half:
- ✅ **"Don't cap green days"** is supported — the P&L genuinely is concentrated in a few days
  (68% of positive R in 10% of months; Breitstein's own "80% of profits from 1–5% of trades" in
  [`risk-management-15-lessons.md`](../../../principles/risk-management-15-lessons.md)). Letting a
  good day run is correct because you cannot know in advance which day it is.
- ⚠ **"Recognize it's the day to go for it and turn it on"** is NOT supported — that is a
  forecasting claim, and the study says the paying periods cannot be forecast. Cameron's own clip
  actually undercuts Breitstein here: Cameron only knew it was the day *after* it had already
  happened, and still gave back $20k.

The **⭐ size-lever study** (2026-09-18) sharpens it further: the lever that worked out of sample
was **exclusion** (trade only A+B grades → +0.29R OOS vs 0.00 flat), **not** a 10× risk spread
(+0.08R with more drawdown). Cameron and Breitstein both advocate the risk-spread version.

### ✅ Real agreements

- **The in-play universe gate** [01:42]. Float <100M + strong daily + high RVOL + catalyst is the
  same construct as [`in-play-stocks.md`](../../../principles/in-play-stocks.md) ("~25 stocks/day
  offer positive EV, fewer than 5 move your P&L") and the same as Cameron's "daily list is usually
  tiny." Third independent source for **selection is the strategy**.
- **High relative volume, with a correction** — rotation study Part II: raise the gate to 1.8;
  **1.3–1.8 is negative**. Cameron is directionally right and most implementations set the bar too
  low.
- **Hard-coded loss limits** [12:40] — matches [`risk-management-15-lessons.md`]
  (broker-enforced daily loss limit) and is the correct operational form. This is Breitstein
  correcting Cameron and being right.
- **Study your own trade data, not just your charts** [11:11] — the whole premise of
  `run_daily_journal.py` and the journal/alert rubric.
- **Shorting parabolic small caps blows people up despite the mean-reversion statistics** [02:26]
  — matches [`crash-leader-study`]: the mean reversion is real and the path kills you, so the veto
  is on the *path*, not the statistic.

### ⚠ Cameron is more cost-aware than Breitstein is

The only mention of slippage in all three of this batch's videos comes from **Cameron**, not
Breitstein: "5 10 cents maybe on slippage getting in, another 5 10 cents on slippage getting out"
[10:33]. On 60,000 shares that is $6,000–$12,000 of friction on a single round trip, and he names
it unprompted. Breitstein's `U9UZ2U6bozQ` and `eWeGAYvjxh4` never mention the spread once. Given
that our studies keep finding the spread *is* the P&L, that is a notable inversion of who sounds
more professional.

### ⚠ Scalability cuts both ways and he only takes one side

At [03:02] he says he abandoned the style because of capacity limits. That is an honest and
important admission — and it is *also* the strongest argument that Cameron's edge is real for a
retail-sized account, since capacity constraints are exactly what protect small players. He raises
it and then never returns to it when arguing the tailwind explains the P&L.
