## Raw notes

**Framing [00:00–01:05].** "Arguably the craziest week since 2020… in many regards actually crazier"
because the speed rivalled 2020 but the cause was policy: "in trading world this all can be erased
with one tweet." Unlike COVID, "there was no putting COVID back into the bottle" — so this event had
"decently binary outcomes" on a headline.

**⭐ The regime switch [01:17–01:46].** *"As a rule of thumb in any high VIX period, you need to
really change your playbook."* He then explicitly **refuses to define the threshold**: "I don't
think it's so important that I define a high volatility period super specifically… most people can
judge it pretty accurately qualitatively." Suggests the VIX index over some level; warns off VXX
("all sorts of decay and weird functions").

**Practice 1 — shorten the horizon [01:59–02:26].** "I'm not trying to have lots of equity on for
large periods of time either way, short or long… because you're opened up to headline risk."

**Practice 2 — go leg for leg [02:26–03:51, 04:27].** "You really want to catch the legs and be
aware that moves can really really reverse hard." ⚠ Embedded claim: roughly ten minutes before the
tariff-pause tweet, "there was just massive massive massive waves of call volume… which I think is
just super scummy." He allows the alternative reading ("maybe it's because we were breaking above
this area") and then dismisses it: "I think probably the reason why we did break above is because of
the flurry of call activity."

**⭐⭐ Practice 3 — the sizing arithmetic [04:27–05:57].** The most valuable 90 seconds in the video:
*"if your risk is generally expanding… if your risk is expanding like this you need to decrease your
size. Now, a lot of people are saying like, oh wait, what do you mean decrease size? Aren't I
supposed to bet more in these periods? Well, yes, in dollar value of risk."* Worked SPY example: you
might normally trade 50,000 shares when the daily range is a couple of points; when the range is
10% (~50 points), 50,000 shares "is a $2.5 million trade." Conclusion: "your share size might be
going down, but that doesn't mean your dollar risk is going down."
⚠ In passing he calls a swing method asinine: "say you're going to be swing trading and using prior
bar lows, which obviously I think is just kind of asinine to do given the ranges and how far away
this is" [04:27] — **which is the exact stop he endorses in `ZOHG-OnQuos`@[04:58]**. Not really a
contradiction: it is a volatility-scaling statement (the prior-bar stop is too far *for his
horizon* in a 10%-range tape), but he does not say so and the two videos read as opposites.

**Practice 4 — don't marry the view [06:10–07:05].** "Markets can reverse so so so so much… so many
people get wedded to certain opinions." He calls the post-tweet NQ rally "such a crazy
overextension… almost seemed like a very very very obvious short," attributing it to "whoever was
front running it and everything else, then the market makers just get offsides and you just get this
mega gamma squeeze."

**Practice 5 — don't overstay without capitulation [07:18–08:52].** "Unless you have a big
capitulation, I think you need to be super careful just how long you overstay your welcome." 4/7:
"this was just classic right side of the V." On taking partials: "I don't know if you necessarily
need to sell there, but I think it's reasonable… this stuff can really reverse way way way more than
you think. **And you can always get back in.** And that's the thing in day trading world —
commissions are cheap, the spread is generally pretty tight." Plus the counter-trend caution: in a
big-picture downtrend, assuming "all the panic's over" after one tweet "is a little bit just kind of
dreaming… we did end up making a move but that was on confirmation."

**⭐ Practice 6 — overnight risk: reduce, don't eliminate [09:14–10:33].** "I touched on the
importance of being careful with overnight risk. I think this is just the truth that most traders
really really find. I found this. Shark finds this. I think we all still mess it up a ton just
because it's so tempting." Then the qualifier that keeps it honest: *"Now some people thought that
means taking no overnight risk. That doesn't mean take no overnight risk or have no overnight
exposure. That means just reduce it and be more aware of the fat tails."* The mechanism he names is
the good part: "the variance, meaning just who knows what overnight headline is coming out, **which
is not in your control and not part of the edge**, is increasing."

**Practice 7 — the daily loss limit [10:33–11:15].** Trillium and SMB enforce one; "even if you're a
trader at home, you should still have a daily loss limit." The operative half is about *when* you
spend it: "what you don't want to do is hit that loss limit too early in the morning and give away
P&L before the actual move happens. So it's so important to **save your bullets**… not to fight the
front side… to wait for the turn… and not to take stupid overnight positions that are then going to
eat into your risk allocation for the next day."

**Practice 8 — selectivity [11:15–12:13].** "In market environments like this, you want to be super
selective. Stuff does and will go so much farther than you think… you really want to wait for the
pocket aces."

**Practice 9 — be present [12:13–12:46].** "You just want your ass in the seat… You don't need to go
out for lunch. You don't need to be fooling around on Instagram." Monitor outside RTH — China
escalations at "maybe 6:00 and 7 a.m."

**⭐ Practice 10 — vehicle selection [12:46–13:20].** "The difference between going into a SPY versus
an SPXL, which is of course a 3x levered, or even going into ES which is futures and levered and
marginable — all of those really really really make massive differences in your P&L on a percentage
basis." Motivation given is explicitly the **retail buying-power constraint**, not directional
expression.

**Practice 11 — game planning [13:20–13:49].** Prep baskets and know the exposure map before the
headline: "do we know what countries RH are most exposed to? Do we know what countries Apple are
most exposed to? What about Nike? What about oil?"

**⚠ Practice 12 — order-entry aggression [13:49–14:12].** "If your keys aren't aggressive enough…
if you're queuing up 5 cents and trying to buy it at 250.75 when there's some massive headline,
you're not going to get it." Prop-infrastructure adjacent: hotkeys, routing, and a willingness to
pay the spread.

**Close [14:12–15:12].** "This is where years can be made… a lot of seven figure days at Trillium
and SMB."

## Reactions / conflicts

**⭐⭐ The sizing arithmetic is correct and is exactly what this repo's harness already does.**
position = risk% ÷ stop% — wider stop, fewer shares, constant dollar risk. It matches
`stops-and-sizing.md` ("volatility widens stops so size down, never tighten"), the 15-lessons rule
("3× wider stop → ⅓ size"), and Luk's near-constant risk. It also matches the repo's own regime
conclusion — **"regime management = fixed small sizing, not a switch"** — on the *sizing* half. He
goes one step further than our harness in that he lets the **dollar** risk rise when the opportunity
set is rich; the size study is the relevant check there, and it found the lever that works is
**exclusion (A+B grades only, +0.29R OOS)**, not a 10× risk spread (+0.08R with more drawdown).

**⚠⚠ Direct conflict: "shorten your horizon / cut overnight exposure" is the single practice our own
data most contradicts.** The exit-timing study found **same-day exits are the negative bucket in
both books** — our pool: scalp −0.13R vs trail +0.89R; Gabe's own log: 278 same-day cycles, −$8.3k,
19% win, and alert-triggered ones no better. The entry study found **buying the daily close beats
every intraday entry** (paired −0.9 to −2.3pp against every intraday variant, t to −3.4). For the
swing book, the overnight hold *is* the return. **Scope is the resolution, not a verdict:** he is an
intraday prop trader with a firm loss limit and no overnight mandate, and he is describing a
one-week binary-headline tape. But as stated the high-vol playbook is **not importable** to this
repo's book, and importing the "shorten horizon" half would move us straight into the losing bucket.

**⚠ Conflict, and a fair one: "change your playbook in high VIX" vs the repo's regime nulls.** Every
trailing-30-day rule derived from August 2026 failed 2019–2026; the paying months are unforecastable;
industry/group RS is not just useless but **inverted** (bottom-3-sector breakouts beat top-3 by
+5.6pp). ⭐ **But his switch is a different object from the ones that failed.** Those conditioned on
*trailing returns and breadth* to predict *payoff*. His conditions on **contemporaneous volatility**,
which is observably persistent (vol clusters; the whole VRP panel rests on it), and he uses it to
change **horizon and size**, not to forecast direction. The repo already has one result where a VIX
state flips a strategy's sign — the SPY short-dated selling pilot, where **VIX<20 is negative**. So
the claim deserves a test rather than the standing null. That test is now specced.

**Complementary, not contradictory, on vehicles.** The August vehicle study found "no vehicle fixes
entries" — same entries expressed as calls / short puts / put spreads all follow entry quality. His
point at [12:46] is about **capital efficiency under a buying-power constraint** (how much exposure
a fixed account can carry), which is a size lever, not an entry-quality claim. Both hold; file them
side by side.

**✅ Agrees with two house rules, in his own words.** "Wait for the pocket aces" [12:09] and "be
super selective" [11:15] are `feedback_precision_over_recall` and
`feedback_conviction_selection_is_the_strategy`. "Save your bullets" [11:04] is the risk-budget
version of the same idea and is the operative content of the daily loss limit.

**⚠ Prop-infrastructure dependency, flagged per README.** Firm-enforced daily loss limit; hotkey and
routing aggression [13:49]; futures access; staffed pre-market coverage; a basket-monitoring setup;
and colleagues posting seven-figure days as the implicit proof. The *concepts* (reduce size as stops
widen, cap the daily loss, be present for the catalyst) are retail-viable; the **execution of them
at his speed is not**.

**⚠ And the retrospective tell.** He calls the rally "a very very very obvious short" [06:23] after
it retraced, with no trigger and no stop given — the same thing his ORB write-up says never to do
("you are waiting for price to prove momentum exhaustion instead of blindly guessing"). One video,
both positions.

**Rating: 3/5** — the best of the three. One piece of arithmetic that is mechanically correct,
repo-consistent and worth repeating (size ∝ 1/stop, dollar risk need not fall); one honest,
well-scoped qualifier on overnight risk; one clean, testable regime claim that survives the repo's
existing regime nulls because it conditions on contemporaneous vol rather than trailing returns. Held
back from 4/5 by: zero numbers beyond a worked hypothetical, a one-week sample recorded mid-event, an
unverifiable front-running accusation, real prop dependency, and a horizon that puts its central
advice in our data's losing bucket.

**Course-marketing content present: yes** — "I might produce this in more formal proper fashion for
Magnum Opus" at [15:00], plus Trillium/SMB credentialling and the "seven figure days" line at
[14:22]. Lighter than most of his uploads; this one reads as a rushed public-service post.
