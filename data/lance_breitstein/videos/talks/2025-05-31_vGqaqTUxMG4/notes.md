Solo. No guest. A Twitter challenge he set — mark the four days on the **daily NQ / NASDAQ chart**

trend and then inform your bias of what might develop over the coming days and weeks" [00:36].

## The four key days, exactly as he defines them

### 1. "4-3" — the definitive break below the double-bottom lows (NQ < 19,000) [01:19–02:23]
Tariffs announced after hours on 4/2; he notes the market "was actually already pretty sold off"
and that the prior leg up was itself a counter-trend bounce. The day matters because of the break,
not the headline: *"once we broke… below these kind of these double bottom lows here below 19,000 in
NQ that is kind of the definitive break to me where this was the start of the second leg lower."*
He ranks it **second** most important.

**Identifiable from daily OHLCV alone? YES.** A close (or low) below the lower of two matched prior
swing lows is fully mechanical; "double bottom" needs a swing-detection rule, which is a few lines.
The only judgement is which swing low counts, and an N-day-low rule removes it.

### 2. April 7 — the capitulation bottom [02:23–04:23]
*"The most important day of this whole chart… this to me was the definitive day."* His stated
ingredients, in his order: one of the fastest down moves in market history, "on par with some of the
down moves in COVID"; "we were extremely far below this lower Bollinger band"; **~20% away from the
moving average** ("this moving average is up at 19,500. The market at the lows on 4-7 was at 16,500
or so… which is almost unheard of for something like the NASDAQ"); "really nice volume"; the number
of consecutive down days; and sentiment — "it felt like the world was ending, the news flow was just
vicious. And all of that tends to lead to a capitulatory bottom." He argues the bounce was going to
happen regardless of the fake tariff-pause headline that day.

**Identifiable from daily OHLCV alone? PARTIALLY — the ingredients yes, the label no.** Distance
from the MA, Bollinger z-score, down-day count and volume are all computable *that evening*. What is
not computable is that it was **the** bottom; "capitulatory bottom" is a post-hoc label on a
condition that also prints repeatedly mid-crash without bottoming. As a **signal** it is testable; as
a **pivot** it is hindsight. ⭐ Note also that he supplies, without noticing, exactly the scale-free
normalization the README demands: 20% from the MA is "unheard of **for something like the NASDAQ**"
— i.e. extension measured against the instrument's own normal range, which is ADR units.

### 3. The post-4/21 gap bar that breaks the downtrend [05:36–06:56]
⚠ **He never says its date.** He calls it "this bar" / "that gap there"; from the description — the
gap immediately after the 4/21 low — this is the 4/22–4/23 gap up on NQ. What he says it does:
*"this bar is to me what breaks this downtrend. Not only do we kind of break this overall downtrend,
but then it kind of cements this here and this 4-21 as a higher low… I easily could have seen this
chart pattern rolling over or at least consolidating in here had we not had that gap there."*

**Identifiable from daily OHLCV alone? PARTIALLY, and only with a lag.** The gap (open > prior high)
and a close above the most recent lower high are mechanical. The **higher low** is not — 4/21 only
becomes a higher low once the *next* pullback holds above it, so the structure he is reading is
confirmable several sessions after the bar he is marking. "Breaks this downtrend" as drawn is a
hand-drawn trendline; substitute "closes above the last lower high" and it becomes testable.

### 4. 5/12 — the gap through 20,500, "game over" for bears [07:08–08:19]
*"The final nail in the coffin… if you're bearish, sorry, you need to stop out. This is you're
wrong."* The bar quality is the whole point: *"look how strongly we broke through that. This wasn't
a doji. This wasn't a bar showing any hesitancy. This was just full throttle."* Consequence he draws
is an action for two different books: if short, you are stopped; if you sold and never rebought,
"you just need to get back in" — and he is explicit that this is **not** a reward/risk claim ("not
that I think the risk-reward is so good, but… if you're short this disproves").

**Identifiable from daily OHLCV alone? YES.** Gap above a prior swing high + full-bodied close
(|close−open| ÷ range above a threshold, close in the top decile of the bar) is a clean daily
condition. The level 20,500 is hand-picked on screen but reconstructs as the prior consolidation
high.

### Honourable mention — 4/9, the official tariff pause [04:37–05:36]
His **#5**, deliberately excluded: "this was the definitive break of trend… we break prior bar
highs, which is one of my definitions of trend" — but he left it out because "I kind of felt that
4-7 was already the bottom anyways." He adds that the extension that day was "driven by a lot of
options flow… the pendulum swinging too far and that was a short up there."

## Named setups appearing here

None, in the KB's sense. **There is no entry, no stop and no size anywhere in this video** — it is a
bias exercise. The four day *types* are a reusable taxonomy (trend initiation on a level break;
capitulation reversal; trend-break confirmation + higher low; continuation gap that invalidates the
opposite case) and that is its value.

**No principles file written.** Deliberate, and the reason is worth recording: (a) he disclaims the
analysis as subjective; (b) n = 1 index over 6 weeks with the outcome known; (c) each mechanical
component is already covered elsewhere — day types 1 and 4 are level/continuation breaks, whose
nearest ledger rows (`LVL pivot break`, `in-play up mover`) both **failed**, and day type 2 is the
capitulation entry now specced in
[`principles/trend-definition-and-counter-trend-entry.md`](../../../principles/trend-definition-and-counter-trend-entry.md).
⭐ **What this video contributes to that spec is the quantification he gives at [03:28]** — the
setup precondition is *extreme distance from the moving average relative to the instrument's own
normal*, which the trend video never supplies.

## Reactions / conflicts

**⚠ The exercise is the repo's forecastability finding, run backwards.** `run_regime_validation.py`
and the breakout-regime feedback study found the paying months **cannot be forecast** from anything
tried — own results, stop-out share, activity, SPY state — with 47% of months positive and the **top
10% of months carrying 68% of all positive R**. April 2025 is precisely one of those top-decile
months. Marking its four pivot days after the fact is easy and tells you nothing about marking them
live; his own honourable-mention discussion of 4/9 is a demonstration of how unstable the marking is
even *with* the outcome in hand.

**⚠ Steenbarger's stat vs the crash-leader study — different objects, do not merge.** The repo's
crash-leader finding is a **veto**: buying deep drawdowns *in a healthy tape* has a median −20% over
252 days, and is a regime bet rather than selection. That study is single-name leaders in a healthy
tape; this is a **panicked index**. The two are not in conflict, and the index version is untested
here. Two repo results lean *toward* him on the index side: the gap study's **high-VIX tercile was
+6.90 bp (t=3.65)** vs −0.83 in low VIX, and the August retrospective found **weak breadth was a
mild BUY, not a sell**.

**✅ Agrees with the capitulation write-up already in the KB.** `capitulation-and-trade-writeups.md`
quantifies a blowoff as range ≥ 2× prior day and volume ≥ 2× prior day after an accelerating run.
4/7 as he describes it is the index instance of exactly that, with the added distance-from-MA gate.
Consistent across two videos two years apart.

**⚠ And it repeats the tension already logged in the ORB principle.** There he insists you never
predict the exhaustion, you wait for the opening range to *fail* first. Here he twice calls an
extension "a short" on the strength of how far it went [05:01, and the 4/9 discussion], with no
trigger attached. The repo's finding is on the side of his ORB self: entries are the whole problem,
and the unconditional fade is dead.

**Rating: 2/5** (honest about its own subjectivity, and the four-day taxonomy is genuinely reusable
— especially day type 2, which hands the capitulation spec its missing precondition. But it is one
index, one window, outcome known, no entry/stop/size anywhere, and the single numeric claim in it is
a second-hand statistic with no sample attached.)
