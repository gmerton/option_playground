# The hot-streak protocol — six proactive steps for when you are winning

> **Verdict:** Process, mostly unfalsifiable, with one claim that can be checked on our own NAV series.
> **Type:** psychology / risk process
> **Source:** `AVoMjAebyB4` — Do THIS After Every Winning Trade (2026-06-06)

---

## The claim

"The most dangerous moment in your trading career isn't when you're losing, it's when you're winning" [00:00]. The cycle [01:06–01:36]: miss the first move, catch the second, size up on the third and fourth, and by the fifth or sixth you are massively oversized "right as the market opportunity is becoming crowded, overextended and lower quality." Example: a friend's best-ever short (CAR after its euphoric capitulation) followed the next day by giving nearly all of it back in HTCO, "a far worse opportunity" [00:36–00:55].

## The six steps [03:18–07:57]

1. **Awareness** — know your personal tell; his is "I oversize subpar setups" during a heater. Set check-ins after N green days, a P&L threshold for the month, or a single day over a threshold.
2. **Hard give-back rules** for the day, week and month, decided in advance — because once attached to the high-water mark you "stop trading the market objectively" and start trading the peak.
3. **Handicap every day and every trade fresh** — "is today's environment actually as good as the previous days, or am I anchoring to recent success?" Opportunities cluster then taper: **after a great day/week/month the next one is probabilistically less good** [05:56–06:07].
4. **Double down on best practices** during the streak — sleep, review, the daily report card — rather than relaxing them.
5. **Reduce noise and stimulation** — no posting winners, no mental spending of unrealized profits; the best traders "become quieter during their biggest runs."
6. **Survival over any single streak** — throttle back late in a cycle to survive enough cycles to compound.

The operating rule under all six: **aggressiveness should match the expected value of the opportunity, not the emotional state of the streak** [03:50].

## ⚠ What is checkable

Step 3 is a testable statement about P&L autocorrelation: the day after a top-decile P&L day should be worse than average. `journal_nav` holds daily NAV from 8/3; with ~25 days it is far too short, but it is the right table and the test is one line once there is a quarter of data. The August retrospective already found the same shape in the *book*: the semis-topping cluster (seven names entered as the sector peaked) is steps 1–3 failing at once.

**Caveats:** anecdote only; Dr. Katz citation is an appeal to authority; course cross-links at [00:36], [02:47], [06:19]. Nothing here is wrong, and none of it is evidence.
