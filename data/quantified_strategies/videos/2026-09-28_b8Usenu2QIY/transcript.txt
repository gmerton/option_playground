The original Darvas concept is almost elegant enough to fit on one line. A stock makes a new high. Price pauses inside a box. Then you buy when it breaks above the top. Darvas wanted strength, consolidation, and then another burst of strength. The problem is not the idea. The problem is precision. Darvas used judgment. A backtest cannot. You need exact answers. When is the top confirmed? How long is the box? How far can price break out before you are chasing it? And what exactly does strong volume mean? So, I kept the heart of Darvas completely intact. Find resistance. Wait for a breakout.

[01:01] Trade with momentum. The big change was simply this. Instead of drawing the box by eye, I defined the resistance level mathematically. Here is the whole entry. First, the box top is the highest high of the previous 12 trading days. Second, SPY must close above it, but not more than 0.75% above it. I do not want to chase an already extended move. Third, volume must be above its 15-day average. Then we buy at the next open. The exit is deliberately simple. When SPY closes above the previous day's high, we sell at the next open. That makes these fairly short trades. The average holding period was about three trading days. Across the full test, there were 279 trades. About 74.2% were winners, and the average trade was plus 0.34%. Good numbers. But this is not the number that made me pay attention. The profit factor came in at 3.08 over those 279 trades. That is strong, but a big profit factor by itself can still fool you.
