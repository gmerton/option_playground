## Entries

- **Never buy a gap-up into a declining 50-day / into overhead supply. Wait for price to reclaim
  the 50 on its own, then buy the back-test.** "Do I buy a gap up into a falling 50 tomorrow? Hell
  no." Missing the first leg is accepted: "there's going to be higher lows along the way… a little
  low-cheat setup before these highs." `2026-09-07_dMuOgHJcYVs@05:44`, `@02:21` (AMD), `@13:44` (TSM)
  (3 mentions / 1 video)
- **Don't chase a 5-minute opening-range break on a gap-up that is the third or fourth up day in a
  row, especially into supply.** "It sounds easy but in real time it makes managing risk a hell of a
  lot harder"; "I'd be a total bozo." `@14:50`, `@17:15` (2 / 1)
- **Preferred entry = morning wash → stops going down → "620 setup" → reclaim of VWAP, so there is
  a low to risk off; or a pullback into the 5/10/20-day with the same trigger.** Require the group
  to be acting well at the moment of entry so price "pushes away from cost quickly." `@08:08`,
  `@15:07`, `@17:40`, `@03:48` (DELL: check-back into the rising 5-day) (4 / 1). **"620 setup" = Gil Morales's 5-minute 6 EMA / 20 EMA cross with MACD(6,20) confirmation; the bullish 6-over-20 cross after an intraday pullback is the entry bar and its low the stop** (definition from Virtue of Selfish Investing; Chart Academy lists the 620 among his core tactics).
- **On gap-up days, the names to watch are the ones that back-test, go flat or red, hold, and turn
  green again — that is the pullback spot inside the gap.** `@12:25` (1 / 1) *(thin)*
- **Level-based triggers when the base is tight: "good above X, below X leave it alone."** OKTA 173,
  SPCX 150, TGTX "take out today's high 56.70" after a two-day low-volume pullback. `@09:03`,
  `@11:49`, `@13:00` (3 / 1)
- **Selection: prefer names that held up best / consolidated sideways through the group's
  correction; ignore straight-up-straight-down names.** AMD's 27% pullback after a ~200% run vs
  POET; DELL went sideways then a catalyst. `@01:53`, `@02:54` (2 / 1)
- **Liquidity check stated per name** ($400M/day SMTC "plenty", $5B TSM, $200M MSFU). `@10:20`,
  `@14:19`, `@07:36` (3 / 1)
