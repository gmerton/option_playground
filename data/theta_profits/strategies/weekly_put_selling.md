Source: `2026-03-15_O5EpCaX4sbA` — "How He Makes 1% Per Week Selling Weekly Puts"
([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O5EpCaX4sbA)). Guest: Josh Walker, Prescott AZ,
electrical/software engineer with a math background, "soft-retired" full-time trader ~2 years
`@02:25`, `@07:50`; host: John. Walker runs a **Patreon** teaching non-complex options strategies
`@41:44` — a mild sales motive, not a course/software funnel.

## Verdict

> **Conviction: 2 / 5 · Risk: 7 / 10 (single-name cash-secured puts, gap/assignment tail) · Tested: NO**
> A disciplined, plausible, and — for this channel — unusually **honest** retail income strategy:
> sell weekly cash-secured puts on tech names, aim for **1.04% of the account per week**, start each
> Monday ~100% cash. The best thing is that he **volunteers the gap between his headline and his
> realized number**: the "1% per week" is a *premium-collection goal* (~1.28% in 2025), but the
> **actual account return was ~0.9%/week → ~30–40%/yr** once you count the assigned losers he was
> still holding `@37:05`, `@38:09`. So the video title oversells by roughly 2×, and he says so. The
> caps: the win-determining step is **discretionary Ichimoku/moving-average level-picking** dressed
> as "robotic math," there is **no separable track record** (self-reported on a small ~$50–60k
> account), the sample is ~2 years of a **benign tech bull regime**, and single-name tech CSPs carry
> a real gap/assignment tail he self-rates at **7/10**. Real structure, honest presenter, zero
> independent evidence — a 2.

## Mechanics

- **Underlying:** individual **tech stocks** (watchlist of ~80–90; "heavy tech… makes me a little
  more risky"). Screens the whole list each Saturday, shortlists 3–6 for the week. `@08:00`, `@08:26`
- **Structure:** plain **cash-secured put** (occasionally a put spread). Sells weekly, wants 100%
  cash on Monday, does **not** want the shares. `@03:03`, `@03:54`, `@04:05`
- **Entry timing:** write **Monday morning** for **Friday** (same-week, ~5 DTE) expiry; if no good
  Monday setup, Tue/Wed for the following Friday. Weekly chosen because theta "dissolves super
  quickly Wed/Thu/Fri" — captures the steep decay, avoids multi-week event exposure. `@03:30`,
  `@04:43`
- **Underlying selection (discretionary):** 1-day **Ichimoku Cloud** for trend/bias — wants price
  **above a green cloud**, bullish conversion/base cross, lag line above cloud; confirms down to
  4h/1h candles. Also likes **IV ~60–80%** for premium (not "too high"). `@08:59`, `@12:19`,
  `@16:41`
- **Strike selection (the crux — discretionary):** switches to **linear-regression channel + 5/10/20
  and 50/100/200 MAs**, treats MA/round levels as "psychological" support/resistance, and writes the
  put **just below a level price "won't pass" that week**; sometimes uses a projected Bollinger 2σ
  band for the far strike. **Explicitly does NOT screen by delta** — only monitors delta to avoid
  extremes. `@17:33`, `@18:03`, `@20:44`
- **Position goal:** **1.04% premium of account value per week** — a *portfolio*-level target, not
  per-trade; pairs a high-premium (higher-risk) name with a low-premium safer one to average to
  1.04%. The **0.04% = risk-free rate (3.6%) ÷ 50 weeks**, added because idle cash would earn it.
  `@22:47`, `@24:20`
- **Profit exit:** designed to **expire worthless** (no fixed PT), but if he's pro-rata ahead
  (e.g. \$800 of a \$1,000 premium banked by Wednesday) he closes early and re-opens Thursday for the
  next Friday to add theta. `@25:30`, `@25:41`
- **Loss management (3 scenarios on/after assignment):** (1) near strike → rest a limit to sell the
  assigned stock at cost; (2) further away → **sell a weekly call** against it; (3) big drop → a
  **put-based cost-basis "stock repair"**: sell **2 puts** halfway between cost and spot + **buy 1
  put** at original cost, immediately exercise the long put to dump the original shares at cost, then
  manage the 2 new short puts — requires staying **bullish**. `@26:34`, `@27:31`, `@29:29`
- **Rolling:** will roll a put out+down for a credit if possible, but says weekly puts have little
  time value left to roll, and **~80% of the time a roll still ends in assignment** — "the roll
  doesn't correct the failed trade, it just makes you feel better." `@32:27`, `@33:16`
- **Sizing:** never more than **~50%** of the account deployed in a week; floors cash at ~20%; some
  bearish weeks he writes **nothing**. `@34:46`
- **Self-rated risk:** **"a six on a good day, an eight on a bad day — call it a seven"**; notes the
  basic put is fundamentally one of the *riskiest* structures (stock→0), reduced only by not going
  naked and by his level analysis. `@35:35`, `@36:39`

## Claimed edge & returns

- **Weekly goal:** 1.04% premium of account `@22:47` — implies ~67%/yr *if* compounded on premium,
  which is **not** the realized figure (see below).
- **2025 premium %:** **~1.28%** of account per week `@37:17` — *above* goal.
- **2025 realized account return:** **~0.9%/week**, i.e. **"between 30 and 40%"** for the trading
  account once you count tech names that dropped and he still held. The pay-the-bills account "right
  under 1%." `@37:33`, `@38:09`
- **Account size:** the bill-paying account was ~**\$50–60k**; primary trading account unspecified.
  `@38:55`
- **No separable, verifiable track record** — no statements, no per-trade log, no third party. All
  numbers are round, self-reported, single-year (2025).

## Objective assessment (where to be skeptical)

1. **The title is ~2× the truth — and he admits it.** "1% per week" compounds to ~67%/yr, but that's
   the *premium-collection* rate on cash; the **realized account return was ~30–40%** because
   assigned losers stay on the books. Credit him for volunteering the gap `@37:33`, but the headline
   is the marketing number, not the P&L number. Any comparison must use the ~30–40% (still
   regime-dependent and unverified).
2. **The edge is discretionary chart-reading, not "math."** Despite the math/engineering framing and
   the word "robotic," the win-determining steps — Ichimoku "5-second tradeable/not" calls and
   picking the level "price won't pass this week" — are **unfalsifiable discretion**. He **refuses to
   screen by delta** `@17:33`, so there is no systematic risk parameter to reproduce. A mechanical
   version is not the strategy that produced the results.
3. **Benign, single regime, tiny sample.** ~2 years full-time, one reported year (2025), a **tech-
   heavy** book in a strong tech tape. Tech CSPs are short a lot of correlated beta; a 2022-style
   tech drawdown (or a single-name gap-down through the strike on earnings/news) is exactly the tail
   this sample hasn't sampled. He himself invokes "the US attacking Iran" `@05:42` as the reason to
   stay weekly — the tail is acknowledged, not survived on the record.
4. **"Stock repair" is doubling down, not repair.** The 2-short/1-long-put cost-basis trick `@29:29`
   only works if you're **still bullish** and adds **net short puts** (200 more shares of exposure)
   into a name that already fell — a martingale-flavored move that lowers cost basis by piling on
   size at the worst moment. Works in a V-recovery tape; compounds the loss in a trending decline.
5. **Rolling is admitted not to work.** By his own number **~80% of rolls still get assigned**
   `@33:16` — so the "adjustment" toolkit is loss-mitigation/cosmetics, not a profit engine. The real
   P&L driver is whether the assigned tech names bounce, i.e. **direction**, which is the thing his
   level-picking claims — but cannot prove — an edge on.
6. **Self-reported on a small account, mild sales motive.** ~\$50–60k, single year, no statements,
   plus a Patreon `@41:44`. Not disqualifying and low-oversell by channel standards, but there is
   **nothing independently bankable** here.
7. **The denominator is honest but the hurdle is thin.** Reporting 1.04% *of the whole account*
   (cash-secured) is the correct capital-at-risk basis — good. But he **builds the 3.6% T-bill rate
   into the goal** `@24:20`, tacitly conceding the alpha must clear risk-free by a wide margin to
   justify single-name gap risk. ~30–40% clears it *if* the self-report and the regime hold.

## What's genuinely sound (the diamond)

- **Honest headline reconciliation.** He proactively separates the **premium %** (~1.28%) from the
  **realized account return** (~0.9%/wk) — the single most credible thing in the interview, and rare
  on this channel. He is not hiding the assigned losers.
- **Correct capital basis.** Cash-secured (not naked) → returns are quoted on **actual account value**,
  the honest capital-at-risk denominator, not "buying power used." No leverage sleight-of-hand.
- **Sober risk self-rating (7/10)** with the correct reasoning that a plain short put is fundamentally
  high-risk (stock→0), and real sizing discipline (≤50% deployed, some weeks zero, keep ~20% cash).
- **Sound theta logic.** Weekly for the steep late-week decay and to minimize multi-week event
  exposure is a defensible, well-articulated reason, not hand-waving.
- **Low oversell:** no "risk-free," no "100% win," admits rolls fail, admits he holds losers, tells
  viewers to adapt rather than copy. Disciplined, plausible retail income process.

## Backtestability

- **Testable skeleton:** weekly (~5 DTE, Monday→Friday) cash-secured puts on a fixed tech basket at a
  **fixed delta** (since he won't name one, sweep ~0.15–0.30Δ), expire-or-assign, with a mechanical
  post-assignment rule (sell the shares / covered call / roll). Measure win rate, per-trade EV,
  realized account CAGR **including carried assignments**, and worst single-name drawdown. That's the
  honest **floor** — it strips the discretionary level-picking, which is the claimed edge, so expect
  it to underperform his self-report by construction (same shape as Time Flies).
- **Not faithfully testable:** the Ichimoku/level "tradeable-or-not" selection and the exact strike
  ("just below the level price won't pass") are **intraday discretionary** chart reads; the stock-
  repair overlay and rolls are discretionary and regime-dependent.
- **Data note:** `silver.options_daily_v3` is **EOD-only** but covers thousands of equities/ETFs with
  greeks + bid/ask, so a **daily-close** weekly-CSP basket on liquid tech names IS approximable — the
  main caveats are (a) the intraday Monday-morning entry/level pick is unrepresentable at daily
  resolution, and (b) single-name coverage/liquidity for his exact tickers must be confirmed
  (Upstart, Paramount Skydance, OpenDoor were mentioned `@09:50`, `@15:17`, `@28:51`). Honest floor:
  the *mechanical* CSP basket is testable; the *selection* that is his edge is not.
