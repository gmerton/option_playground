## Mechanics

- **Underlying:** liquid equities he thinks **could double** off a support bounce / breakout —
  explicitly Chewy (CHWY) and Palantir (PLTR) `@07:06`, `@25:19`. Liquidity matters because it's a
  multi-leg, weekly-rolled structure; he admits his examples (CHWY) already carry **~30¢ short-leg /
  ~40¢ long-leg spreads** `@07:38`, `@07:49`.
- **Structure — call ratio diagonal (the Chewy trade, entered Nov 4, stock ~$33):** `@05:38`–`@06:18`,
  `@13:07`
  - **Sell 2× near-dated slightly-OTM calls** — 34 strike, Nov 14 expiry (~10 DTE). All extrinsic
    since stock < strike; collected ~72¢ = "**2% a week** in extrinsic decay." `@09:33`, `@09:46`
  - **Buy 3× longer-dated further-OTM calls** — 35 strike, March 20 expiry (~100+ DTE). `@06:11`,
    `@09:51`
  - **Ratio 2:3** (sell 2 / buy 3) — the extra long call is what keeps net delta positive; the
    difference in strikes/dates makes it a *diagonal*. `@04:39`, `@13:07`
  - **Net Greeks he targets: positive delta ~+72** (≈ synthetic 72 shares) **and positive theta** —
    "as high [delta] as possible while maintaining positive theta decay." `@08:39`, `@09:08`
  - **Debit paid: $915** (later quotes total risk as ~$1,100–$1,140). `@13:35`, `@14:50`, `@18:39`
- **Entry timing / conditions:** ATM/near-money setup after a support bounce; wants **short-dated ATM
  IV elevated enough** to make the weekly extrinsic worth collecting; doesn't screen on individual-leg
  deltas, only the **net** position delta+theta. `@08:02`, `@08:15`, `@08:39`
- **Profit target:** discretionary — take profit "whenever I want" if the stock **breaks out / goes to
  $40–$50**; otherwise keep collecting weekly roll credits. `@11:03`, `@17:48`, `@18:05`
- **Stop / max loss:** **the debit** — "all calls, so if the market goes down I lose whatever debit is
  paid." No stop; treats a down move as "no different than owning a long call." `@11:28`, `@20:46`,
  `@21:00`
- **Adjustments (weekly rolls):** roll **only the short calls** each week when their extrinsic is gone
  (out ~1 week, same or higher strike) for a credit (collected ~$100, $112 in the shown weeks); the
  **March long calls never move** (keeps commissions/complexity down). If the stock **drops**, he may
  **skip** writing the short calls that week; on an up-move he **rolls the shorts up** (one ITM / one
  OTM) to shrink the upside "valley." `@11:56`, `@12:28`, `@15:15`, `@16:49`, `@17:00`
- **The upside "valley of death":** because of the 2:3 ratio, a **moderate** up-move to around the
  short strike at the near expiry can lose **more than the debit** — a real (bounded) upside risk he
  acknowledges but downplays, saying his broker's chart (which counts residual extrinsic in the March
  longs) looks "more positive" than his own absolute-loss chart. `@14:03`, `@14:26`, `@16:49`
- **Variation — add a long put (COP example, entered ~$86, rose to $93):** makes it "a long strangle
  with positive theta," but **IV fell as price rose and the put lost**, netting **~breakeven** on a
  trade the diagonal part won. He calls it **experimental and a drag**. `@19:03`, `@19:43`, `@20:22`
- **Sizing:** small — "small allocation plays… total risk ~$1,100 in a relatively large account…
  if I lose all of this it's nothing." `@18:39`
- **Self-rated risk: 4/10** — defined risk, positive theta, "very little management." `@21:27`

## Objective assessment (where to be skeptical)

1. **No separable track record — and the one number shown is unrealized.** "14% in 44 days /
   annualized 120%" is a **mark-to-model on a still-open** trade whose thesis (a *double*) hasn't
   happened; annualizing an open winner off a $1,100 base is textbook overselling. The corpus of
   evidence is **~6 self-reported trades in one benign 6-month window** — nowhere near enough to
   estimate a win rate or EV, and impossible to audit.
2. **The edge is stock selection, which he admits.** "This is **not** the strategy for [someone] who
   has no edge in guessing the direction" `@23:23`. The structure is a *packaging* of a bullish bet;
   the P&L is dominated by whether CHWY/PLTR actually breaks out. The "income" and "positive theta"
   are second-order sweeteners, not the driver. So any backtest of the *skeleton* mostly measures your
   ticker picks, not the trade.
3. **Costs land exactly where it hurts.** He wants **high-breakout small/mid-caps** — the same names
   with **30–40¢ spreads** — and the mechanics require **selling and buying every single week** (roll
   the shorts) across multiple legs. Weekly commissions + bid/ask on wide chains plausibly eat much of
   the "2%/week / ~$100 per roll" he's collecting. He never nets returns against this; the ROC figure
   is a mid-price mark.
4. **The "positive theta" advantage evaporates in the down case — exactly when you'd want it.** Theta
   is positive **only while the short calls hold extrinsic** (stock near/below the short strike). If
   the stock **falls meaningfully**, the shorts decay to ~zero, there's nothing left to sell, and by
   his own words the position becomes "**no different than owning a long call**" `@11:36` — i.e. a
   decaying long-vol bet. The theta selling point is conditional on the stock cooperating.
5. **The upside "valley" means max loss > debit.** He calls it defined-risk = debit, but the 2:3 ratio
   creates a region on a **moderate** up-move where losses at the near expiry exceed the debit; he
   relies on **residual extrinsic in the March longs** (a model value, and IV-dependent) and active
   **rolling-up** to keep it small. Defined and bounded — yes — but the clean "max loss = debit" line
   is an at-expiration / model simplification. `@14:03`, `@16:49`
6. **The honest COP result undercuts the "it also works with a put" pitch.** He's candid that adding
   the long put turned a winner into ~breakeven because **long puts lose vega as the stock rallies**.
   Credit for saying so — but it means the "long-strangle-with-positive-theta" variation is, on his
   own tiny sample, a **negative** contributor.
7. **"Probability of profit is quite high" is close to vacuous.** POP is high because the near-term
   short calls cover small moves — but the *reason to do the trade* (a double) is a low-probability
   tail; high POP on a structure whose thesis is a rare big move mostly means many small
   grind-along outcomes while you wait, with the debit as the floor.

## What's genuinely sound (the diamond)

- **The core construction is legitimately clever.** Financing a long-dated OTM "lotto" call's theta by
  writing *more* near-dated calls than you're long, sized to keep **net delta positive and net theta
  positive simultaneously**, is a real, thoughtful improvement over both a naked long call (bleeds
  theta) and a PMCC (caps upside / carries more downside). It **lowers the cost basis** of a
  convex up-bet — a genuine structural insight, not marketing.
- **Downside is cleanly defined** — all calls, shorts fully covered by longs (2 ≤ 3), no naked short
  vol, no assignment blow-up; the true worst case is the (bounded) debit-plus-small-valley.
- **Unusually honest presenter for this channel:** volunteers the losing put variation, the wide
  spreads on his own examples, the requirement for a directional view, small position sizing, and that
  he's shown only ~6 trades. Soft (free-content) sales motive, no course/software funnel.
- **Positive theta while the stock ranges is real** and is the honest differentiator vs a plain long
  call — you get paid to wait for the breakout instead of bleeding for it, *as long as the stock
  doesn't fall away from the short strikes.*

## Backtestability

- **Skeleton is approximable on our EOD data** — `silver.options_daily_v3` covers thousands of
  equities with full greeks + bid/ask, so a mechanical "sell 2 near-OTM calls / buy 3 longer-OTM
  calls at a 2:3 ratio, roll the shorts weekly, exit at +X% or on an N% breakout" can be simulated at
  the daily close on a fixed liquid universe (CHWY/COP-class names). Not futures — no issue here,
  it's equities.
- **The honest floor / why the test would be weak:** (a) the **alpha is ticker selection** (naming the
  stocks that double), which a mechanical universe can't reproduce — the backtest would mostly measure
  the chosen list; (b) the returns hinge on **tiny weekly roll credits (~$100 on wide 30–40¢ chains)**
  that **modeled multi-leg fills at mid will overstate** — mark-at-mid/settle-intrinsic flatters
  exactly this kind of thin, high-turnover, wide-spread structure; and (c) the **discretionary roll
  decisions** (skip the shorts when it drops, roll up to shrink the valley) are not mechanically
  specifiable. So a skeleton test sets an **upper bound** that his real (discretionary, cost-laden)
  version would sit below.
- **Not faithfully testable:** the stock-selection edge, the weekly discretionary roll management, and
  the intraweek "should I write the calls this week" calls.
