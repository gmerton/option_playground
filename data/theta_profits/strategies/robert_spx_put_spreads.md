# ATM SPX Put Credit Spreads (Small-Account Growth) — Robert Mintosh

Source: `2025-12-28_55ox9fB3x-E` — "How Robert grew a $3,200 account selling SPX put spreads"
([watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=55ox9fB3x-E)). Guest: Robert Mintosh (auto-caption spelling;
likely "McIntosh"), central Oklahoma, ~20 yrs off-and-on across stocks/futures/forex but a **brand-new
options trader — started March 2025**; host: John. **No sales motive** — no course, software, Discord,
or newsletter is pitched; he's just a listener sharing a run. That's a genuine plus versus the channel
norm, but it does not add independent evidence.

## Verdict

> **Conviction: 2/5 · Risk: 5/10 (defined-risk, but ATM gamma + aggressive small-account sizing + weak
> stops) · Tested: NO (mechanical skeleton is highly testable)**
> An honest, unpretentious small-account story: sell **at-the-money** SPX put credit spreads, 10 points
> wide, 5–7 DTE, entered discretionarily on EMA pullbacks, mostly held to expiry. The best thing about
> it is the honesty — he self-rates a **3**, names *over-leverage* (not the structure) as what has hurt
> him, admits he "should be better at stop losses," and flatly says he does **not** expect to keep
> 5×-ing. The worst thing is that the headline — **$3,200 → ~5× in ~4 months** — is a textbook
> survivorship/leverage path: a tiny account run aggressively on **ATM (≈50-delta) short spreads**
> through a benign 2025 bull tape, where "71% win over ~50 trades" is mostly **P(market didn't drop this
> week)** plus drift, not a demonstrated edge. Selling ATM makes this a **directional bet on bull
> continuation dressed as income**. Defined risk and no sales motive keep it off the floor; zero
> separable evidence and heavy regime dependence keep it at 2.

## Mechanics

- **Underlying:** SPX only (cash-settled, no assignment, hold to expiry for full premium). `@00:44`,
  `@20:07`
- **Structure:** short **at-the-money** put + long put **10 points lower** (a 10-wide put credit
  spread). SPX 10-wide = **$1,000 max value**; at ATM he collects **~$3.50–$3.80 credit (~$350–$380)**
  → **max loss ≈ $650**, a ~2:1 (risk:reward just under 2:1). Occasionally goes 15-wide. `@04:30`,
  `@04:44`, `@05:37`, `@16:34`
- **DTE:** **5–7 days.** Rationale: most of the decay lands "in the last day / day-and-a-half"; further
  out, credit thins and margin is tied up longer. `@04:44`, `@05:10`
- **Entry (discretionary):** looks for **pullbacks on the daily chart** relative to the **20 EMA**
  (guideline, not a hard rule). Rule of thumb: if SPX is **>1% above the 20 EMA** it's "probably" due to
  pull back; enters into down/pullback days. On a **hard** extension he waits for a couple of pullback
  days (not the first); if price drops **below** the 20 EMA he waits for the **first recovery day**.
  Sometimes checks the hourly on expiry day just to gauge intraday speed. `@06:01`, `@06:32`, `@07:39`,
  `@09:49`, `@11:05`
- **Frequency / expiry spacing:** ~**one trade/week** on average (sometimes two); likes expirations
  Thu–Mon; will stretch a trade to two weeks only to avoid stacking multiple expiries in the same week.
  `@06:58`, `@12:10`, `@18:15`
- **Exit / stops:** prefers to **let it expire** if well OTM (saves a commission, keeps full premium).
  If it's going against him on expiry day he'll "let it ride till about noon" hoping for a bounce, then
  take a "**one-to-one loss**" (~1× the credit). Admits he is inconsistent about stops: "I should be
  better at doing stop losses because it has caught me a few times." Rarely rolls — usually just closes
  and re-enters (the close often becomes his next entry). `@13:10`, `@13:37`, `@13:48`, `@14:46`
- **Sizing (aggressive by design):** single contract on ordinary down days; **adds a contract or
  increases size on a deeper (~1%) pullback**. Runs 1–3 spreads, usually 2 overlapping. Explicitly
  accepts a **~10% of account max drawdown** ($650 × 2) as the price of growing a small account fast;
  says he'd "never go that high" on a six-figure account. Plan: bump contract size every +$10k, aim
  ~$50k by end of summer, then shift to preservation. `@07:09`, `@17:16`, `@18:36`, `@23:44`
- **Self-rated risk:** **3** (3–4 for a beginner, ~2 for a disciplined/experienced trader). "Risk is
  well defined… you only have to worry about the market going in one direction." `@19:18`

## Claimed edge & returns

- **~50 trades since June, 71% win rate** (self-reported, ~4 months). `@19:52`, `@20:07`
- **$3,200 (March) → ~$12k account, "made a little over $18k"** (has withdrawn cash, hence the two
  numbers) — "just under 5×." `@00:00`, `@03:52`, `@04:05`
- **Averaging ~$130/day** vs a **$100/day** target; "**June and July were not good months**, the others
  really good." `@20:07`, `@20:22`
- All numbers are **self-reported, unverifiable** — no statements, no per-trade log, no third-party
  audit. Sample is **~50 trades over one benign 4-month window**.

## Objective assessment (where to be skeptical)

1. **The headline is a survivorship + leverage artifact, and he half-admits it.** $3,200 → 5× in ~4
   months on a small account is exactly the outcome you'd expect to *see interviewed* out of many
   parallel small-account punts; the ones that halved don't get a video. He says himself he does **not**
   expect to keep 5×-ing (`@23:44`) and that aggression is only "acceptable on a really small account"
   (`@18:48`). The **percentage return is inseparable from the aggressive sizing** (10%/trade-cluster
   drawdown tolerance), not from a per-trade edge.
2. **"71% win" on ATM spreads is close to P(no meaningful drop), not evidence of alpha.** An
   **at-the-money** short put is ~50-delta; a 5–7-DTE ATM put credit spread wins whenever SPX is flat-
   to-up over the week. In a persistent 2025 uptrend, a 71% weekly win rate is roughly **drift +
   his pullback-timing**, not a demonstrated repeatable edge. The structure is a **thinly disguised
   directional bet on the bull continuing** — he even says the S&P "wants to go up over time" is what
   makes it work (`@10:15`). Flip the regime and both the win rate and the path invert.
3. **Thin, regime-fragile EV.** At $350 credit / $650 max loss, break-even win rate is
   650/1000 = **65%**; his 71% clears it by ~6 points → EV ≈ **0.71×350 − 0.29×650 ≈ +$60/trade
   gross**, *before* commissions and *before* the fact that ATM spreads breach their short strike
   easily. A modest drift in win rate (a choppy or down quarter) pushes this negative fast. He confirms
   the fragility: **June/July were losing months** and late-Oct/early-Nov "went south pretty hard."
4. **ATM = maximum gamma/pin risk, and he holds into it.** Keeping a short strike *at the money* into
   expiration day — his stated default ("let it expire… keep the full premium") — is the single
   highest-gamma way to run a spread; a 1% adverse close flips a full winner to a full loser. His
   mitigation is discretionary ("let it ride till noon"), i.e. intraday hope, not a rule.
5. **Weak, self-admitted stop discipline.** "I should be better at doing stop losses because it has
   caught me a few times" (`@13:48`). The defined-risk cap ($650) is the only true backstop; between
   entry and that cap there is no consistent rule, so realized losses cluster near max on the bad weeks.
6. **Over-leverage is the real tail — by his own account.** The two times he got hurt were **too many
   contracts expiring in one bad week** (`@17:16`, `@17:28`). Adding contracts into deeper pullbacks
   (`@08:58`) *increases* size precisely when the market is already falling — the wrong-way sizing that
   turns a defined-risk structure into a cluster of correlated max-losses.
7. **Discretionary entry is unfalsifiable.** The "1% above the 20 EMA → wait for a pullback / wait for
   the first recovery day" timing is by-feel, admittedly "ambiguous" (`@06:32`), so the realized win
   rate can't be cleanly attributed to the rule vs. luck vs. drift.
8. **Confused denominator.** "$12k account but made $18k, pulled some out" — normal for a live account,
   but it means the clean, auditable return series doesn't exist; the "5×" and "$130/day" are back-of-
   envelope, not a reconciled track record.

## What's genuinely sound (the diamond)

- **Defined risk, cash-settled.** 10-wide caps the per-trade loss at ~$650, SPX settles to cash (no
  assignment, no early-exercise), and he deliberately keeps the spread tight to hold ~2:1 and ~10%
  account drawdown. That's a real, bounded structure — no naked short, no undefined tail.
- **Honest, low-oversell presenter with no sales motive.** He self-rates a sober **3**, names
  over-leverage (not the strategy) as his failure mode, concedes weak stop discipline, flags June/July
  losses, and explicitly disclaims that the 5× pace continues. Nothing is being sold. That candor is
  why this sits at 2, not lower.
- **Regime-appropriate reasoning.** Choosing 5–7 DTE to capture the back-loaded decay, tight spreads to
  survive a full loss in two trades, and SPX for cash-settlement/no-PDT-friction are all defensible
  small-account choices.

## Backtestability

- **Highly testable skeleton — one of the more testable strategies in this KB.** SPX is fully covered
  in `silver.options_daily_v3` (2010→2026-02) with greeks and bid/ask. The mechanical core —
  **ATM put credit spread, 10 points wide, 5–7 DTE, held to expiry (or a 1× stop)** — maps cleanly onto
  EOD data. We can measure win rate, EV after modeled 2-leg fills (mark at mid, settle intrinsic), and
  the full-sample vs. bull-only-vs-bear split that the interview can't provide.
- **The honest floor:** two things are **not** faithfully testable at daily resolution — (a) the
  discretionary **EMA-pullback entry timing** (the claimed skill), and (b) the **expiry-day intraday
  management** ("let it ride till noon," hourly-chart exits). A mechanical version therefore tests the
  *skeleton*, not his hand-management, and will likely differ from his discretionary record.
- **The critical null comparisons:** (1) ATM put spread with **no timing** (enter on a fixed cadence)
  vs. his pullback entry — does the EMA rule add anything over drift? (2) vs. simply being **long SPX**
  over the same window — does the premium structure beat buy-and-hold in a bull, and how badly does it
  lose in 2022/Aug-2024/Apr-2025 drawdowns? (3) ATM vs. lower-delta (25Δ) short strike at matched DTE —
  quantify how much of the loss frequency is the ATM choice.
