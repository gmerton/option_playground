## Mechanics

- **Underlying:** /ES ($50 × S&P 500) and /NQ ($20 × NASDAQ-100) futures options; favorite is
  **/NQ** for its richer premium and higher volatility ("the wild child") `@11:57`, `@12:29`.
  Recommends beginners start on micros /MES and /MNQ (1/10 size) `@06:39`, `@19:44`. Futures
  options are **physically settled into one futures contract on assignment** (not cash-settled)
  `@11:41`.
- **Core structure:** **put credit spread** — sell a put below "put support," buy a further-OTM
  put to define risk and cut margin; sometimes just a **naked short put** (~$36K margin on /NQ)
  `@13:24`, `@14:10`, `@18:40`. Undefined-risk naked puts are explicitly part of the toolkit.
- **DTE:** **0DTE** — futures have **daily expirations**; she sells "every single day" for daily
  income `@14:24`, `@14:37`.
- **Strike selection (discretionary, no delta rule):** does **NOT** use a fixed delta `@14:49`.
  Maps technical levels on the chart, then overlays **gamma exposure (GEX)** to find call
  resistance / put support, and sells the put below put support `@13:24`, `@14:10`. Tools:
  **Tanuki Trade** and **OptionsDepth** for GEX; translates **SPX/QQQ** GEX (deeper, more liquid)
  onto /ES and /NQ `@15:30`, `@16:17`. Collaborates with "Doc McGraw" (a GEX interviewee) `@17:13`.
- **Entry timing:** trades ~23 hours/day across Asia / London / US sessions; most active US hours
  with a daily plan posted off DEX levels `@00:57`, `@29:36`, `@30:05`.
- **Profit target / stop:** none stated as a rule. Repeatedly stresses "always know your max
  loss" `@31:44` but gives no fixed PT or hard stop; the defined risk is the spread width.
- **"Repair" ladder (the crux):** `@21:18`–`@24:07`
  1. **Take assignment** into a long futures contract, then sell it (or a covered call) once price
     recovers above break-even.
  2. **Short the future intraday** at a level above break-even when she's "fairly confident" the
     strike will breach; the eventual assignment (long) covers the short.
  3. **Roll** the credit spread out in time, down in strike, for a credit.
  4. Else **close for a loss**.
- **Sizing / self-rated risk:** account ≥$50K for /ES /NQ, else micros `@27:16`. Risk framed
  entirely as **leverage: "you live and die on the sword," 13.9x /ES, 13.7x /NQ; don't
  oversize** `@08:35`, `@17:38`, `@28:43`.

## Objective assessment (where to be skeptical)

1. **77% in four months is an extraordinary claim on a soft, paywalled denominator.** "$77K on a
   100K average account" is unauditable (her own weekly spreadsheet, inside a paid Discord). A
   ~77%/4-month clip annualizes to numbers that only come from very high leverage or very high
   tail exposure — which directly contradicts her own repeated "don't oversize" thesis. Extreme
   return + short window + sales motive is the exact profile the skeptic mandate flags.
2. **"91% win rate" is vacuous on credit spreads.** Selling OTM 0DTE put spreads wins whenever
   price doesn't fall through the short strike — i.e. the win rate is just **P(no large down
   move)** and says nothing about EV. The economics live in the ~9% of days that breach, where a
   0DTE credit spread can lose several multiples of the credit collected. A high win rate here is
   the *design*, not evidence of edge.
3. **The "repair strategies" are discretionary directional bets, sold as free saves.** Shorting
   /NQ intraday to cover a put she thinks will breach `@22:15` only works if the down-move
   continues. On a **whipsaw** (she shorts at 25,100, price reverses up) she books a loss on the
   short *and* the put expires fine only after she's already realized the hedge loss — the repair
   can **compound** the damage, not cap it. She narrates only the clean win `@25:51`. This is
   "admitted no edge on direction, but a claimed edge in adjustments/feel," the KB's classic red
   flag.
4. **Naked short puts are in scope** `@18:40` — undefined-risk, ~$36K SPAN margin per /NQ put,
   physically assigned into a leveraged long future. That is a genuine tail, not a defined-risk
   book.
5. **13.9x leverage + 0DTE + physical assignment is a structurally high-risk vehicle,** and she
   says so honestly — but honesty about the risk doesn't reduce it. SPAN margin can expand fast; a
   gap through the strike on a leveraged future is exactly the "steamroller" case.
6. **"NASDAQ-100 is one of the safest things you can do" is a sleight of hand** `@28:28`. Index
   *survival* (it won't go bankrupt) is not the risk here — the risk is a **fast intraday move
   through the short strike on 13.9x leverage** before expiry. The index recovering "in a day or
   two" is cold comfort to a 0DTE position that already settled against you.
7. **Un-reproducible discretionary core.** GEX-level reading + "no fixed delta" + intraday feel +
   cross-instrument (SPX/QQQ GEX → /ES /NQ) translation means the trade cannot be specified as a
   rule, so the win rate is inseparable from unspecifiable skill and can't be replayed.
8. **Sample is ~4 months.** Even taken at face value, that is far too short to characterize a
   short-premium tail; the loss distribution is defined by rare days that a 4-month window likely
   under-samples.

## Backtestability

- **Untestable on our stack — hard null.** `silver.options_daily_v3` does **not** contain
  **futures options (/ES /NQ)**; the entire strategy is on instruments outside the table, so it
  cannot be tested on current data.
- **Even with the data it would be intraday-uncapturable:** it is **0DTE** with **discretionary
  GEX strike selection** and **intraday directional repair (shorting the future)** — our table is
  **EOD-only (daily bars)**, so same-day entry/exit, intraday hedges, and the assignment/short
  interaction are unrepresentable. This is the honest floor: **not worth building.**
- **Crude cash-index proxy only:** a mechanical **0DTE (or 1DTE) put credit spread on SPY/QQQ/SPX**
  at a fixed short delta could be run on the table as a *distant cousin*, but it would strip (a)
  the GEX selection that is the claimed edge, (b) the intraday repair ladder, and (c) the
  futures leverage/SPAN economics — so it would test a different, generic strategy, not hers.
  Refer to the existing 0DTE reviews (`pulver_0dte.md`, `zerodte_breakeven_iron_condor.md`) for
  the thin-edge-vs-costs economics of that family.
