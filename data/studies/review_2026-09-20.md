# Same-day review of the 2026-09-20 session — are we discarding profitable strategies?

Asked by Gabe after a day of ~20 studies, almost all negative. That is exactly when a systematic
bias toward nulls could operate, so each conclusion was re-examined for the false-negative case.

## Three findings changed on review

**1. PEAD — the ledger's best row was NOT killed. Re-labelled PARKED.**
The write-up said the tape-reaction earnings signal "does not replicate on 2× the names" and was
"momentum in an earnings costume". That conflated a wider universe with more data on the same one:
the original 241 names were **100% the straddle pool**. Split by pool membership on the *independent*
yfinance event set, the signal reproduces on the pool at **edge +0.244R (original +0.246), t 1.89,
both halves positive**, and is weaker off it. Universe-specific, not spurious. The surprise-based
PEAD null stands. Ledger: 0/50 passes, one PARKED near-miss standing.

**2. Short earnings vol — positive after costs on liquid names. Re-labelled PARKED.**
The premium study called it NULL after costs; the gates study noticed `avg_volume` helped and filed
it as "a cost proxy". Re-examined with a day-clustered t: top 40% by volume, **selling at the bid,
+0.284%, 7 of 8 years positive**; ≥5M avg volume +0.281%, and **+0.438% at a mid−25% limit fill**.
t ≈ 1, so below the bar — but it is a real, consistent, after-cost positive that was under-reported.

**3. Straddle stop — the "intraday would be worse, upper bound" claim was wrong in direction.**
An intraday stop triggers more often but exits nearer −50% rather than the daily-close overshoot to
−64%. On the stopped cohort, a perfect −50% fill less the exit spread is ≈ −55% vs −54.75% held —
roughly neutral. The no-stop conclusion **holds** (exit spread, 20-cell sweep, independent 2026-09-10
result), but the confidence language over-reached and is corrected.

## What survived re-examination unchanged

- Debit call spread as a vehicle (bad-entry book; both data limits flatter it, still last).
- Event convexity as a debit spread (friction t −5.51; cheap far-OTM geometry, not Ravish's).
- FTD index and names (names labelled UNDERPOWERED at 8 episodes — already honest).
- Entry vs stop (2.6 ADR of location — a mechanism, not a discard).
- Retrace entry (PARKED — already honest).
- Hold/fail predictors (9 features, univariate + simple combos; the +1.27R cohort is unknowable from
  what is in the book — scoped correctly).
- Straddle slippage (ADOPTED, positive).
- Stop sweep (0/20; the gradient is robust to the mark caveat).
- calculator.py vol gates (iv30/rv30 inert and inverted; slope tested on both vehicles).
- Earnings calendar (one geometry only — next expiry, ~7d gap; longer back legs untested, noted).

## Two caveats that apply across the earnings work

- **Fill assumption.** "At the bid" is the conservative arm. The mid−25%-of-spread arm is what a
  patient limit order often gets on liquid names, and it flips the liquid cell from +0.28% to +0.44%.
  The verdicts were set on the conservative arm; the parked cells should be re-run at both.
- **The calendar was one geometry.** Short front / long *next* expiry (median 7-day gap). oquants and
  tastylive often use a 30–45 day back leg, which is a different vega/gamma mix. Not tested.

## Net

No adopted strategy was discarded. Two negatives were over-stated and are now PARKED with the
numbers that justify it. One confidence claim was reversed. The session's central finding — that
friction, not the idea, decides these outcomes — is unchanged and, if anything, sharpened: the one
earnings cell that survives is defined by liquidity.
