# Does O'Neil's Follow-Through Day time the turn? (2026-09-20) — FAIL

**Queued from** the TraderLion scan: Ariel Hernandez (2026-09-13) builds regime entry on the FTD,
citing O'Neil/Webster for ~30% invested within a day or two of it. Our own regime work says trailing
regime state has no persistence and that regime management is fixed small sizing, not a switch — an
FTD is a dated, rule-based switch, so it is a sharp test of that.

**Verdict: the FTD does not time the turn.** It adds nothing at the horizons the claim lives at, it
fails outright on IWM, and the rule barely beats a trivial version of itself.

**Method.** `run_ftd_study.py`. SPY 1993–2026 (8,467 sessions), QQQ 1999–, IWM 2000–, yfinance daily
with volume. Canonical rules: correction = close below (1−DD)× the 252-day rolling max; rally attempt
day 1 = the session holding the lowest close so far, and **a new lower close resets the count** (the
undercut rule); FTD = day 4+ of the attempt, close up ≥ THRESH, volume above the prior session. Only
the **first** FTD of each correction is scored. Four parameter cells (DD 5/8/10%, threshold 1.0/1.7%).
Significance is a **bootstrap** — forward windows overlap heavily and there are only 16–37 signals, so
the null draws the same number of days from the same pool 10,000 times and inherits the overlap.

## 1. Against other days in the same correction — the actual claim

Edge in pp (bootstrap p):

| SPY | DD5/1.0 | −0.10 (.57) | −0.25 (.63) | +0.17 (.45) | +2.86 (.06) |
| SPY | DD8/1.0 | +1.26 (.08) | +0.28 (.41) | +0.54 (.38) | +3.72 (.09) |

| QQQ | DD8/1.0 | +0.57 (.31) | +1.14 (.23) | −0.15 (.53) | +7.25 (.03) |
| **IWM** | DD5/1.0 | **−0.84 (.89)** | +0.08 (.49) | −0.05 (.53) | **−1.05 (.70)** |

At +5 to +21 days — where "be 30% invested within a day or two" lives — SPY and QQQ show nothing
consistent, and **IWM is negative in 10 of 12 cells**, with p up to 0.97. IWM is the index where
O'Neil-style small/mid leadership actually lives, so failing there is not a footnote.

## 2. Does the rule beat just waiting four days?

The sharpest control: the **naive arm** takes the first day ≥4 of the rally attempt and ignores the
up-% and volume conditions entirely. FTD minus naive, in pp:

| index | +5d | +10d | +21d | +63d |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPY DD5/1.0 | +0.24 | −0.14 | −0.34 | +1.75 |
| SPY DD8/1.0 | +1.69 | −0.05 | −0.89 | +2.60 |
| QQQ DD5/1.0 | +0.69 | −0.11 | +0.01 | +2.21 |
| IWM DD5/1.0 | −0.77 | −0.06 | −0.95 | −1.66 |
| IWM DD8/1.0 | −0.77 | −1.14 | −2.31 | −3.22 |

**At +10d and +21d the rule adds nothing or is negative on every index.** Whatever the FTD captures
at those horizons is "time since the correction low", not the up-% and volume gates. Those gates are
decoration.

## 3. The one thing that is not nothing

+63d on SPY/QQQ survives every control (+1.8 to +6.3pp over naive, p .01–.05 vs all days). But that
is the wrong shape for the claim. A 63-day window opened from inside a drawdown is mostly measuring
**"drawdowns recover over a quarter"**, which needs no FTD to exploit and which our crash-leader study
already classifies as a *regime bet, not selection*. It also inverts on IWM. It does not support
getting invested within a day or two; it supports the market mean-reverting over a quarter.

⚠ **Multiple comparisons.** 12 cells × 4 horizons × 3 comparisons ≈ 144 tests. At p<0.05 roughly
seven false positives are expected by chance, and that is about how many appeared.

## Verdict and what it changes

**Nothing.** The desk routine keeps fixed small sizing; no FTD switch is added. This is the third
independent failure of trailing-regime timing in this book (trailing 30d rules, breakout regime
feedback, now FTD), and the pattern is consistent: **the tape's recent state does not forecast the
next stretch, however the rule is dressed up.**

Worth stating plainly: O'Neil's FTD may still be useful as *permission to stop being defensive* —
a psychological trigger to re-engage after a decline. This test says only that it carries no
measurable forward edge over waiting a few days, and none at all on small caps.
