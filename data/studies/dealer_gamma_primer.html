Dealer Gamma Primer

What positive and negative gamma mean, how dealer hedging plays out across a trading day, and which parts of the popular story our own tests support.

Underlying: SPY (QQQ as robustness) Tested sample: 2010 → 2026-02 All times ET
1 · One option

Gamma for a single option

  • Delta is how much an option's price moves per $1 in the underlying. A 0.50-delta call behaves like 50 shares.
  • Gamma is how fast delta itself changes as the underlying moves.
  • Long an option means positive gamma. Your position gets longer as the stock rises and shorter as it falls, so big moves either way help you.
  • Short an option means negative gamma. Your position moves against the market: shorter as it rises, longer as it falls. Big moves hurt.
  • Gamma is largest for options near the money and close to expiry.
2 · The other side

Why dealers matter

Market makers take the other side of customer option trades and earn the bid-ask spread. They don't want a directional bet, so they delta-hedge: they buy or sell the underlying, mostly ES futures and SPY, to keep their net delta near zero. Which way that hedging pushes the market depends on the sign of their gamma.

Dealers long gamma GEX > 0

Market rises → they sell. Market falls → they buy.

Hedging leans against moves: dampening, mean reversion, range days.

Dealers short gamma GEX < 0

Market rises → they buy. Market falls → they sell.

Hedging chases moves: amplifying, trend and expansion days.

This is the same split Al Brooks draws between trend days and range days. The difference is that the gamma sign is known before the open.

3 · Measurement

How we estimate it

Nobody can see dealer positions directly. The standard naive assumption is that customers mostly sell calls (covered calls, overwriting) and buy puts (protection), which leaves dealers long calls and short puts. The desk computes exactly that in run_gex_fly_paper.compute_gex:

Net GEX = Σcalls (OI × γ) − Σputs (OI × γ)
        × 100 × S² × 0.01   → dollars per 1% move
every SPY expiry · strikes within ±20% of spot S

Reading the number: −$7.8bn per 1% means that if SPY moves 1%, dealers would need to trade roughly $7.8bn of SPY exposure in the same direction as the move to stay hedged. A positive number means trading against the move.

2026-09-24, morning (intraday estimate)−$5.87bnper 1% · SPY 765.97
2026-09-24, 13:14 ET (intraday estimate)−$7.82bnper 1% · SPY 768.40

Why it often turns negative in selloffs: as the market falls, spot moves toward the large put open interest below it. Those puts gain gamma, and under the naive sign dealers are short them, so net gamma goes negative just as the market gets fragile.

4 · Sequence

A trading day, from the dealers' side

Customers are retail traders, funds buying puts, institutions selling covered calls, volatility funds, and a growing crowd of 0DTE traders. Dealers are firms such as Citadel Securities, Susquehanna and bank desks, which hedge with ES futures, SPY and stock baskets.

  1. ~6–7 am

    Open interest is published

    The OCC publishes how many contracts exist at each strike as of yesterday's close. ES futures have traded all night, and dealers can hedge there.

    For GEX: OI updates only once a day, so any GEX number is yesterday's book priced at today's spot.

  2. pre-open

    News sets the gap

    Overnight news moves ES. The gap decides where spot opens relative to the big strikes.

    For GEX: gamma depends on spot's distance to the strikes, so a gap alone can change the regime.

  3. 9:30

    The open

    Opening auction and the heaviest volume of the day. Customers open new positions, including 0DTE trades that won't appear in anyone's OI until tomorrow.

    For GEX: this is the biggest blind spot. 0DTE is a large share of SPX volume, and the morning's new positions change dealers' real gamma in ways the pre-open estimate can't see.

  4. through the day

    Continuous re-hedging

    Dealers re-hedge in bands, when their delta drifts past a threshold, rather than on every tick. Long gamma means selling rallies and buying dips; short gamma means the reverse.

    Two secondary flows move delta without any price change. Vanna: a change in implied vol changes delta. In a rally IV usually falls, the delta of the puts dealers are short shrinks, and they buy back hedges, which extends the rally. Charm: time decay changes delta, most strongly into the close, Fridays and expiry.

    For GEX: vanna and charm are general market-structure theory. We have not tested them. See the appendix for an introduction to each.

  5. ~2 pm – close

    Gamma peaks

    Near-expiry options carry their highest gamma, and 0DTE contracts head to zero or intrinsic by 4:00.

    For GEX: hedging is most intense and most sensitive to small moves. Pinning talk centers here, and our pin test failed.

  6. 3:50 – 4:00

    The close

    Market-on-close imbalances and the closing auction. Leveraged ETFs rebalance in the direction of the day's move, which acts like one more short-gamma flow.

    For GEX: the desk's certified 1-day trades price here, which is why the GEX close step runs from 15:30 and the paper fly enters around 15:50.

  7. after 4:00

    Expiry and the overnight count

    SPY options expire daily. The day's positions are tallied for tomorrow morning's OI.

    For GEX: tomorrow's number starts from here, and the prior-close sign is the variable our tests use.

6 · Limits

Caveats

  • The sign is assumed, not observed. When customers pile into calls, as in 2021, dealers can be short calls and the naive number reads the wrong way.
  • Dealers hedge whole books across many products, some of them off-exchange, so SPY option OI shows only part of their position.
  • Intraday readings are not the tested number. Today's −$5.87bn and −$7.82bn were mid-session estimates; the validated variable is the reading at the close.
  • The effect is on how much the market moves, not which way. SPY and ES trade enormous volume, and dealer hedging is one flow among many.
7 · Practice

The practical rule

Positive gamma at the close: selling short-dated index premium has paid.

Negative gamma at the close: expect bigger moves than implied vol suggests. Don't sell premium and don't fade moves.

The tradeable part is sizing premium sales to the regime, not predicting direction.

Appendix

The second-order flows: vanna and charm

Gamma describes how a dealer's delta changes when price moves. Two other sensitivities change that delta when price stands still: a change in implied volatility (vanna) and the passage of time (charm). Both produce hedge flows, and both depend on the same naive positioning assumption as GEX: dealers long calls and short puts, with most of the open interest in out-of-the-money puts. Neither has been tested in our ledger. What follows is the standard market-structure account.

A.1

Vanna: delta that moves with implied volatility

vanna = ∂Δ / ∂σ   (equivalently ∂vega / ∂S)
  • What it measures. How much an option's delta changes when implied volatility changes, with price unchanged.
  • The intuition. Higher IV makes an out-of-the-money option more likely to finish in the money, so its delta grows in size. Lower IV shrinks it toward zero. An OTM put's delta goes from, say, −0.20 to −0.28 when IV jumps, and back toward −0.12 when IV collapses.
  • The dealer flow. Dealers short OTM puts are long delta from them and hedge by being short the underlying.
    • IV falls (typical in a rally): the puts' deltas shrink, dealers are now over-hedged short, and they buy back hedges. This is the "vanna rally" that can extend a calm grind higher.
    • IV rises (typical in a selloff): the puts' deltas grow, dealers are under-hedged, and they sell. This adds to the decline, alongside the negative-gamma flow.
  • When it is largest. Around big vol moves: the day after an event such as FOMC or CPI (when IV is crushed), and during volatility spikes. It is concentrated in OTM options with some time left.
A.2

Charm: delta that decays with time

charm = ∂Δ / ∂t   ("delta decay")
  • What it measures. How much an option's delta changes as one day passes, with price and IV unchanged.
  • The intuition. As expiry approaches, an OTM option's chance of finishing in the money shrinks, so its delta drifts toward zero. An ITM option's delta drifts toward ±1.
  • The dealer flow. With the market steady, the long delta dealers carry from their short OTM puts decays every day. They become over-hedged short and buy back hedges. The flow is steady and directionless in cause, but under the naive assumption it is a bid.
  • When it is largest. Close to expiry and into the close, when the most time decay is realised. Also into weekends, since two days of decay are priced on Friday, and around monthly expiration (OPEX) when large positions roll off. After a big expiry, the flow that supported the market can disappear, which some traders associate with post-OPEX weakness.
