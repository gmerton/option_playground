# PEAD on the actual surprise (2026-09-20) — surprise NULL; the tape signal is PARKED, not dead

> ⚠ **CORRECTED 2026-09-20 (same-day review).** The original write-up concluded the ledger's near-pass
> "does not replicate on 2× the names" and was "momentum wearing an earnings costume". That conflated
> two changes: the original 241 names were **100% the straddle pool** (liquid, weekly-optionable,
> high-vol), and the wider set is a *different* universe. Split by pool membership on the NEW
> (independent, yfinance) event set, the tape signal **reproduces on the pool** — edge **+0.244R**
> vs the original +0.246, t 1.89, both halves positive (+0.094 / +0.245) — and is weaker off it
> (+0.073R, t 1.01). That is **universe-specific**, not spurious. Verdict for the pool cohort:
> **PARKED** (below the t bar, right direction, consistent, reproduced on an independent event
> source). The surprise-based PEAD conclusion (NULL) is unchanged. `pead_pool_check_2026-09-20.log`.

**VERDICT: NULL · METHOD.** Post-earnings drift measured on the real EPS surprise does not work here.
More importantly, the closest-to-a-pass pattern in a 50-row ledger turns out to be **momentum wearing
an earnings costume**, and it does not survive a wider universe.

**Why this was the top candidate.** `earnings drift, good+MUTED` (2026-09-19 rerun) scored
**+0.272R, edge +0.246, t 2.652, both halves positive** — two of three bar criteria, failing only
|t| ≥ 3. It looked like a power problem. The study itself flagged three fixable limits: coverage of
**241 of 1,743** names, no EPS-surprise data (so "good" was proxied by the tape's reaction), and
unknown AMC/BMO timing. All three are now fixed by `run_earnings_calendar_pull.py` (yfinance):
**12,232 events on 456 names**, 96% with surprise, and a known report hour.

## 1. The market prices earnings surprise about right

`corr(surprise, reaction) = 0.203`. Reaction-day return: **beat >10% → +2.54%**, inline → −1.00%,
miss <−10% → −2.05%. A large beat buys you two and a half percent, once, on the day.

And the surprise-based version of the old near-pass — big beat, muted reaction — is **negative**.

## 3. The decomposition: it was the tape, not the surprise

Same events, same wider data, the **old tape-reaction definition** (up day, volume ≥1.5×, upper-half
close):

| definition | n | meanR | edge | t | halves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2026-09-18 original (241 names)** | 437 | **+0.272** | +0.246 | 2.652 | +0.186 / +0.327 |
| same definition, 457 names | 873 | **+0.060** | +0.082 | 0.94 | **−0.058** / +0.139 |
| surprise-based equivalent | 2,446 | −0.030 | +0.087 | −2.12 | −0.035 / −0.026 |

**On the broad panel the near-pass weakens to +0.060R** with the first half negative. ⚠ But see the
correction at the top: restricted to the straddle-pool names it reproduces at edge +0.244R on an
independent event set. What it measures is an up-day-on-volume-with-a-strong-close near an earnings
date — a tape signal, not the surprise — and that signal appears to be real on liquid high-vol names. `good_tape` events carry a median surprise of +10.2% against +5.8% otherwise — related,
but at correlation 0.20 that is not the same variable.

## METHOD — two lessons worth more than the result

1. **A proxy that correlates 0.20 with the thing it proxies is not measuring it.** The old study was
   explicit that "good" was a stand-in for surprise. It was a stand-in for something else.
2. **Do not treat a near-pass on a narrow universe as a power problem until the universe is widened.**
   The obvious read — t 2.65 on n=437 just needs more events — was wrong. More events made it worse,
   which is the diagnostic that separates a real effect from a small-sample one.

## What changes

Surprise-based PEAD comes off the list. The tape-reaction signal on the **straddle pool** stays
**PARKED** — it is not earnings drift in the academic sense, but it reproduced on an independent event
source at the same edge. The ledger's honest record: 0 of 50 passes, **one PARKED near-miss standing**.
