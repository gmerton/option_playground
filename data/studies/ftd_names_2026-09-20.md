# Ariel's actual FTD claim: do single names work better after one? (2026-09-20)

**Correction to the first test.** `ftd_2026-09-20.md` asked whether the *index* rallies after an FTD.
That was the wrong target. Ariel says plainly **"most follow-through days tend to fail"** — he agrees
the index is unreliable. His claim is about single names: *"immediately after a valid follow-through
day, you get stock after stock after stock after stock really going to work."*

**Verdict: the claim fails, but not the way the index test implied.** The post-FTD window really is a
better time to be long — and the breakout entry is a *worse* way to express it.

**Method.** House breakout (close > 20-session high, ADR ≥ 3, eligible), the same mask as the
regime-split study, split into breakouts firing within N sessions of a SPY first-FTD and all others,
each run through the standard harness with its own honest `post` control (random later session, same
name). Panel 2019–2026, 44,062 breakouts. **8 FTDs fall inside the panel window.**

## Result

Best arm per window, meanR (edge over its own control in brackets):

| window | POST-FTD | OTHER | diff |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5d | **−0.196** (−0.241) | −0.072 (−0.100) | **−0.125** |
| 10d | −0.081 (−0.138) | −0.074 (−0.078) | −0.007 |
| 21d | **−0.142** (−0.212) | −0.067 (−0.068) | **−0.075** |

Post-FTD breakouts are **worse on every window and every exit arm**, and they miss their own control
by more (−0.138 to −0.266) than ordinary breakouts miss theirs (−0.046 to −0.100).

## The interesting part

The **controls** tell the real story. For post-FTD names the random-later-session control is
**+0.045 to +0.080R**, against **+0.000 to +0.028R** for the rest. So the names breaking out after an
FTD *are* better names — entered at some random later date they do measurably better than the norm.

What fails is the entry. Buying those names **on the breakout** returns −0.08 to −0.20R while simply
owning them at a random later moment returns +0.05 to +0.08R.

So Ariel's "all boats are being lifted" is directionally supported — post-FTD is a good window to
carry exposure. His prescription is not: **the breakout is the wrong way in during that window, and
more wrong there than usual.** That lines up with the entry study (close entry beats intraday, chasing
hurts) and the alert-funnel result (the signal marks a state, not a moment).

## ⚠ Power

Only **8 FTDs** sit in the panel window (2019-01-15, 2020-04-06, 2020-06-16, 2022-03-15, 2022-05-04,
2023-10-06, 2023-11-01, 2025-03-19). The 900–3,815 breakouts in each window cluster inside those 8
episodes, so the **effective sample is 8 events, not thousands** — the printed t-stats (−0.2 to −2.2)
do not account for that clustering and are optimistic. This is enough to say there is no sign of the
large positive effect claimed; it is not enough to pin the size of the negative one. Extending the
panel before 2019 would be the way to settle it, and the index test says the prize is small.

## What changes

Nothing on the desk. But the useful carry-forward is sharper than "FTD fails": **after a correction
turns, the post-FTD window is worth having exposure in — just not by chasing breakouts into it.**
