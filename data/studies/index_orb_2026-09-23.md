# Index opening-range breakout on SPY and QQQ [WL-5a] — 2026-09-23

**Verdict: NULL · YIELD METHOD.** On 19 years of SPY and QQQ 1-min bars, the direction of the opening-range break
is worth nothing: primary vs the same minute taken the opposite way, SPY −0.001pp (t −0.08), QQQ +0.006pp (t +0.28),
2026 hold-out ≈ 0. The ORB itself loses about the round-trip cost (−0.02 to −0.03% per trade). This closes the
scope gap the ORB review raised: our ORB nulls now cover single names **and** the index.

Script: `run_index_orb.py` (pre-registration in the docstring, from `data/orb_backtests/README.md`).
Log: `data/studies/logs/index_orb.log`. Trades: `logs/index_orb_{SPY,QQQ}_primary_trades.csv`.
Local, ~minutes. Data: `data/cache/intraday_hist/{SPY,QQQ}_1min.parquet`, 2007-01 → 2026-09-17. The verdict
window is 2007–2025; 2026 was held out and read only after the verdict was fixed.

## Spec

- **Opening range:** 15 minutes (09:30–09:44).
- **Signal:** the first 15-minute bar closing outside the range, at or before 11:59. Enter at the next minute's open,
  in the direction of the break.
- **Stop and exit:** stop at the other side of the range; exit at +1.5R or the 15:59 close.
- **Costs:** 1bp per side + $0.005/sh; stops fill one tick through; a bar that touches both stop and target counts
  as the stop.
- **Metric:** % per trade.

  hold-out is flat.

## ⚠ Control (b) is contaminated; don't read it

The registered secondary control (b) is the same direction at a random minute from 09:45–12:00. That window includes
minutes **before** the break, and the direction comes from a break that happens later. That's hindsight, the same
defect as the old `month` control. It "beats" the ORB by t −5 to −13, which is the look-ahead, not a finding. The
primary control (a) has no such problem: same minute, only the direction flips. Future random-minute controls for
breakout-type triggers should draw only from minutes **after** the signal.

## Reading

- On the index, the opening-range break carries no directional information after costs. The same answer as the single-name
  ORB9 / ORB15 / ORH nulls, now on 19 years and ~9,000 index sessions.
- Trading Steady's own S&P backtest (flat 2020–22) and Raghee's "most fail" are consistent with this. Fit Mom's NQ
  version (futures, New York session) isn't directly covered, but SPY/QQQ is the same underlying at the same hours.
- The only index intraday signal still standing here is the noise-band momentum (MARGINAL, its negative-gamma cell
  t 2.92), which is a volatility-scaled band, not a fixed opening range.
