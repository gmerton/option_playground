# Stage A — do the intraday long triggers have an edge? (2026-09-18)

**Answer: no.** 11,227 long alerts over 158 sessions and 182 symbols. Every intraday exit arm is negative, and
**no arm beats a random entry in the same symbol on the same session**. The one positive arm (hold it as a swing)
also loses to that control, and is positive in only one half of the sample.

## Method (pre-registered before the run)

- **Signals:** every UR / ORB9 / LVL fire, regenerated by replaying the *current* detector code over all 164
  cached sessions (`run_stage_a_replay.py`, tag `stagea`) so one rulebook covers the whole sample. Shown **and**
  suppressed (out-of-play) alerts, because the gate is one of the things being tested.
- **Entry** next 1-min bar's open + 10 bps. **Stop** the level the alert published. `R = (exit − entry) / (entry − stop)`;
  stop fills 10 bps through, targets fill at the level, and a bar that could be both is scored as the stop.
- **Arms:** stop/close · 1R · 2R · VWAP loss · 30-min time stop · next day's close · swing trail (alert stop on a
  daily close, then first close under the 20 EMA, cap 60 sessions).
- **Control:** 5 random entries per alert in the same name-day (09:45–15:30) at the same dollar risk, same arms.
  This is the strong control: it already contains the "this name is in play today" information.
- **Split:** Feb–May vs Jun–Sep. **t** clustered by session date.
- Scripts: `run_stage_a_replay.py`, `run_stage_a_score.py`; trades in `data/cache/stage_a_{trades,control}.parquet`;
  full output `stage_a_intraday_2026-09-18.log`.

**By detector** (mean R, intraday stop/close → swing trail):
- **ORB9** (n=2,246): −0.246 → +0.461. Worst everywhere; t −5 to −12 on the intraday arms.
- **UR** (n=8,296): −0.082 → +0.502.
- **LVL** (n=685): −0.011 → +0.890, the only detector that is roughly flat intraday, and the thinnest sample.

**Split sample** (mean R): swing trail **+1.251 Feb–May vs −0.268 Jun–Sep**; every intraday arm negative in both.

**The in-play gate adds nothing.** Shown −0.115R vs suppressed −0.109R intraday; swing trail 0.46 vs 0.54. It
shortens the list without separating outcomes. Nor does the daily state: LONG −0.133, SHORT −0.122, OUT −0.101,
FLAT −0.084 on the stop/close arm.

**Time of day** buys nothing either: 09:00 −0.085, 10:00 −0.116, 11:00 −0.191, midday ≈ −0.10. The swing-trail
column rises through the day (09:00 +0.39 → 13:00 +2.10) but on 171 observations by 13:00, and it is the arm the
control already beats.

**Capacity:** 71 signals per session. Median risk 1.37% of price.

## Reading

1. **The trigger carries no information over the name-day.** A random minute in the same stock on the same day
   did as well or better in every arm. Whatever the detectors surface, it is "this name is moving today", which the
   daily scan already tells us.
2. **This is the intraday restatement of the house finding** that selection is the edge and intraday machinery is
   execution insurance ([[project_ibkr_bot]]'s "the gate cannot buy precision", the alert engine's own
   "pattern surfacer, not an edge").
3. **Do not fund Stage B for these detectors.** Five years of Polygon data would measure more precisely something
   that already fails its control at n = 11,227. Spend that only on a *different* hypothesis.
4. **What would change the verdict:** a far more selective trigger (71/session is a firehose), pre-registered and
   tested out of sample — not a sweep over the same pool.

## Limits

Mechanical exits only; a discretionary trader may do better, but this data cannot show that. One regime
(Feb–Sep 2026). 10 bps slippage, applied equally to signal and control. The universe is the alert universe, which
was itself chosen by past scans.
