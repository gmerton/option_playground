# August 2026 Retrospective — what actually worked

*Built 2026-09-05 off the Minervini day-cache (5,302 common stocks; liquid universe = ADDV ≥ $30M and price ≥ $5 at 7/31, n=1,765), Tradier ETF history (87 ETFs), yfinance sector/industry map (1,712 of 1,765 mapped). Scripts: session scratchpad `retro.py`, `retro2.py`, `retro3.py`.*

## Headline

August 2026 was a **laggard-squeeze month wrapped in a two-week index rally**. The index gains were made in the first four sessions and given back from 8/14 on. The money was in groups that were 30–60% off their highs on 7/31 (gold/silver miners, crypto, genomics/biotech, cloud software, copper/uranium). Names that qualified as leaders on 7/31 — the Trend Template cohort and the preferred list — finished the month flat to down. Standard momentum entries (50-day and 52-week breakouts, pullbacks to the 20 EMA, U&R) worked only in the first half and lost money in the second half. The one setup family that worked in both halves was **breakouts/squeezes in beaten-down names**.

Readings:
- **Breakouts of leaders were a coin flip all month**; RVOL didn't separate winners from losers in August (RVOL 1.8–2.5 bucket −0.9%, ≥2.5 +0.3%). The 52-week-high variant was the worst momentum entry (28% win in H2).
- **Breakouts of laggards were the only breakout cohort that worked in both halves**, and improved as the tape weakened. Consistent with the 2006–2026 finding in `industry_rotation_detection_study.md` Part III (volume-confirmed breakouts in bottom-3 sectors beat top-3 by ~6pp/63d).
- **Pullback-to-20-EMA and U&R are regime trades**: profitable while the 10-day advancer average was above 50%, money-losing after 8/18.
- **Chasing +10% gap days was negative** in the thrust phase (crowded, faded), only worked late in the month with tiny n. Shorting −10% gap days was wrong in H1 (they bounced +2.6% over 5 days).
- **Short side**: breakdowns on volume only paid after 8/17 (30% of them were higher 10 days later). Failed breakouts had no edge as shorts. Shorting rallies into the 200-day in broken names was the worst trade of the month (+4.6% against, 60% of the time).

## 5. The "optimal" August, reconstructed with ex-ante information only

1. **8/3–8/4 breadth thrust (72%/75% advancers, new highs tripling)** → go long. Not SPY. Buy the groups that are ≥30% off their highs and had started turning in July: gold/silver miners, bitcoin proxies, genomics/biotech, cloud/application software, copper and uranium miners. An equal-weight basket of GDX / IBIT / ARKG / WCLD bought at the 8/4 close returned **+19.7%** to 8/31 vs SPY −0.6% from that close and the preferred list −1.5%.
2. **Single names**: laggard breakouts on ≥1.5× volume (ASST, U, TWST, TXG, ABCL, WPM, CRCL, TEAM in the first half; MSTR, AMR, WIX, CRCL again in the second) and "July-turner deep laggards" (the n=51 cohort, +13.6% for the month, +11% by 8/21).
3. **8/18 (10-day advancer average < 50%, second straight sub-35% day)** → stop initiating momentum longs, hold only the groups still making highs (miners, crypto, biotech), and flip the breakdown screen on: volume breakdowns below the 50-day low from 8/17 on lost another 2.6% in 10 days.
4. **Avoid all month**: leader breakouts to 52-week highs, extended names into earnings (the bottom-40 list is nearly all of these), semis after the H1 bounce (SOXL +26% H1, −22% H2), defense (XAR +12% H1, −14% H2), airlines, regional banks, retail.

## 6. Implications for the machinery — ⚠ REVISED 2026-09-05 after validation

The three proposals originally written here (a laggard-turn candidate screen, a breadth gate on pullback/U&R entries, and "stop initiating longs when the 10-day advancer average drops below 50%") were encoded as rolling rules and tested on 2019–2026 in `trailing_regime_validation.md`. **None survive**: the laggard-vs-leader spread does not persist (corr −0.11), laggard breakouts lose in 7 of 8 years (−1.35pp excess), the breadth gate does not separate outcomes, and weak breadth has preceded *higher* 21-session returns. The August pattern was real; it was not forecastable from the trailing 30 days.

What stands:
- The preferred list / Trend Template universe was on the wrong side of August, and there is no validated rule that would have told you so in advance. That is a regime-diversification statement, not a screen.
- The existing vetoes (no breakouts below the 200-day or with 6-mo return < −10%; no chasing +10% gap days) are confirmed by the long sample.
- Group RS tables ranked semis, defense, and insurance highly on 7/31 (63-day RS). All three lost money in August. Descriptive, not predictive — same as the rotation study said.
- `run_trailing_retro.py` reproduces this study for any trailing window as a post-mortem tool; it prints its own non-persistence numbers.

## Caveats

One month, so all setup numbers are small-n and regime-specific; they describe August, they do not forecast September. The H1/H2 split is ex post, but the breadth signals that define it were visible in real time. Laggard/leader cohorts are defined at 7/31, so those are true ex-ante cohorts. 53 of 1,765 names have no industry mapping. Forward returns use closes, no slippage, no stops; the MAE column in the scratchpad output shows the median 10-day worst excursion was −4 to −5% for most setups, so a tight stop would have changed the arithmetic materially.
